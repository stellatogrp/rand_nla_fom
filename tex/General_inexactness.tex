\section{Introduction}
In this paper we consider the problem of a maximal monotone inclusion on a real Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$:
\begin{align}
    \text{find } x \text{ s.t. } 0 \in\Acal x, \label{eq:monotone-inclusion}
\end{align}
where $\Acal:\Hcal\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}$ is a maximal monotone operator.
% These problems have been well-studied for over 50 years, going back to the original work of \cite{Martinet1970RegularisationDV}.
A popular class of methods for solving this problem is the proximal point method (PPM): for a stepsize $\gamma$, we try to solve the backwards inclusion $x_k \in (I + \gamma\mathcal{A})x_{k+1}$.
PPM has a long history, going back to the original work of \cite{Martinet1970RegularisationDV}, but it more well-known in the English-speaking world through Rockafellar's pioneering work on the subject (\cite{rockafellar-ppm-paper,rockafellar1976monotoneoperatorsandppm}).
The operator $(I + \gamma\mathcal{A})^{-1}$ is single-valued and defined everywhere whenever $\Acal$ is maximal monotone (Minty's theorem, see \cite[Theorem 21.1]{Bauschke2017FinerPropertiesOfMonotoneOperators}) and is known as the \emph{resolvent} of $\mathcal{A}$.
It is known that PPM includes general classes of operator splitting methods, such as Douglas--Rachford (\cite{EcksteinBertsekasDouglasRachfordSplitting}).
For an introduction to the theory of monotone operators and their application in these methods, see \citet{Ryu_Yin_OptimizationMonotoneOperators}.
The intuition is that by regularizing via $\gamma$ and solving the inclusion problem, a previously difficult problem may be decomposed into a sequence of simpler operations that are easier to implement.
This broad idea finds many applications in convex optimization, with notable success stories such as ISTA, FISTA, and PDLP and its variants \citep{ISTA-original-paper,FISTA-original-paper,applegate2022practicallargescalelinearprogramming-pdlp-paper}.

It is a curious and important fact in algorithm design that the resolvent computation need not be done exactly.
\cite{rockafellar-ppm-paper} first showed that it suffices to compute a sequence of iterates whose errors form a summable sequence.
\cite{EcksteinBertsekasDouglasRachfordSplitting} extended the result to show that in fact, Douglas-Rachford is an instance of PPM and therefore it once again sufficed to compute iterates whose approximation errors were summable.
However, the choice of such a sequence forms another parameter for the optimizer to tune, making practical implementations difficult.
Moreover, the theory does not provide meaningful guidance on the design of such a sequence, nor does it provide guidance on how the choice of tolerance affects the work done over the course of the algorithm.

To alleviate this difficulty, it would be nice to have a \emph{relative} error criterion: one where the tolerance is a function of the current state of the algorithm (e.g.\ the fixed-point residual of the iterates).
This would provide meaningful guidance on the difficulty of solving a particular subproblem, as well as making tuning the algorithm easier.
\cite{EcksteinRelativeError2018} provide an affirmative answer to the question of designing a relative error criterion for Douglas-Rachford and ADMM, and their result was extended by \cite{svaiter2018weaklyconvergentfullyinexact,BarrePEPInexactMethods}.

More recently, \cite{bredies2021degeneratepreconditionedproximalpoint} showed that many existing operator splitting methods, such as Chambolle-Pock, Douglas-Rachford, Peaceman-Rachford, and Davis-Yin, are instances of \emph{degenerate preconditioned} proximal point methods.
That is, we precondition the monotone inclusion problem \Cref{eq:monotone-inclusion} by replacing the scalar stepsize $\gamma$ with a matrix $M\succeq 0$ and evaluate $(I + M^{-1}\Acal)^{-1} = (M + \Acal)^{-1}M$.
These ideas can be extended to relatively inexact resolvent evaluations, such as in \cite{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting}.

However, an analysis of the \emph{inner complexity}, the complexity of computing $x_{k+1}\approx (I + M^{-1}\Acal)^{-1}x_k$, and its relationship to the convergence of PPM is more delicate. %has been missing in the literature.
\cite{machart2012optimalcomputationaltradeoffinexact} considers the complexity of inexact proximal gradient methods, but they only consider the case of absolute errors, with the standard summability conditions.
Moreover, they only consider a specific instance of an algorithm, whereas our setting is general enough to cover many more operator splitting methods.
\cite{LinMairal2018CatalystAcceleration} considers a model of global complexity, factoring in inner and outer complexity, but their error criterion is not generally computable, requiring knowledge of the exact solution to the subproblem or access to the dual function.
\cite{alves2017iterationcomplexityinexactdouglasrachford} provides analysis in a similar setting but provides only asymptotic guarantees of inner complexity.

\subsection{Setting and Notation}
We assume we have access to a linear map $\Ccal:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ which we refer to as a \emph{degenerate preconditioner}.
For an operator $\mathcal{S}$, we define the set $\zer \mathcal{S} = \{u : 0 \in \mathcal{S} u\}$.
We define the resolvent of an operator $\mathcal{S}$ as $J_{\mathcal{S}} = (I + \gamma\mathcal{S})^{-1}$, and its fixed point residual operator as $R_{\mathcal{S}} = I - J_{\mathcal{S}}$.
In general, we will not use $R$ or $J$ to refer to operators other than $B$ defined in \Cref{def:b-definition}, so we drop the subscript and use the standing assumption that the underlying operator referred to is $B$.

An unspecified norm $\|x\|$ refers to either the standard Euclidean norm or the Hilbert space norm of the ambient space $\mathcal{H}$.
We denote the adjoint operator with a $*$, i.e. $\langle \Ccal u,v \rangle = \langle u, \Ccal^* v\rangle$.
For Euclidean space, this coincides with the transpose of a matrix, which we denote with $\top$.
We use $\log_+ := \max\{0,\log\}$ to denote the positive part of the logarithm.

The variables $u,w,y$ and $z$ are always used in a way that matches their use in \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}.
The reader should always assume that we are trying to find $u,w,y$ and $z$, such that $\Ccal z \in \Acal u$, $\gamma z + \Ccal^* u \approx w$, and $y = \mathcal{C^*}u$.
The symbol $\gamma$ is always used to refer to the proximal parameter, also referred to as the stepsize.
Similarly, $\sigma$ will always be used to refer to the inexactness parameter of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}, and $b$ refers to the interval between inexactness checks.

We make the following assumption to ensure that all the operations we take in this paper are well-defined.
\begin{assumption}\label{assumption:well-defined-resolvents}
    We assume that $\Acal$ is a maximal monotone operator $\Acal: \mathcal{H} \rightrightarrows \mathcal{H}$ on the Hilbert space $\Hcal$ with at least one zero: $\zer \Acal \neq\varnothing$.
    We assume that the map $\Ccal:\Dcal\to\Hcal$ is a bounded linear map between Hilbert spaces, that $\Ccal^*$ is surjective, and that $(\Ccal\Ccal^* + \gamma\Acal)^{-1}\Ccal\Ccal^*$ is defined everywhere.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}\label{def:b-definition}
Define the operator $B$ by its graph as
\begin{equation}
    \gra{B} = \{ (\Ccal^* u, z) : \Ccal z\in\Acal u\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
% For simplicity, assume that $\Acal u = \nabla f(u)$, where $f$ is a smooth convex function, and assume $\Ccal = I$ for simplicity.
% Then, the operator $B = \Acal = \nabla f$, and the analysis below reduces to that of the proximal point method on a smooth convex function.
% To connect this with the discussion above, suppose $\Ccal\Ccal^* = M \succ 0$ and $A$ is single-valued.
% Then, in item 2 of \Cref{lem:B-properties}, we can see that $Cu = Mv + \gamma Cz$
% To get more intuition behind the definitions, see \cite{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting}.
Since $\Ccal^*$ is surjective (\Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents}), the resolvent $(I+\gamma B)^{-1}$ is defined on the entire space $\Dcal$ (\Cref{lem:B-properties}).
Note that $B$ itself need not have full domain, since $\operatorname{dom} B = \Ccal^*\operatorname{dom}\Acal$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:B-properties}
    For $B$ defined as in \Cref{def:b-definition}, under \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents}, the following hold
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\zer{B} = \Ccal^* \zer{\Acal}$.
        \item For $x\in\Dcal$, $w = (I+\gamma B)^{-1}x$ if and only if there exist $v\in\Hcal$ and $z\in\Dcal$ such that $x = \Ccal^* v + \gamma z$, $\Ccal z \in \Acal v$, and $w = \Ccal^* v$. Moreover, such $v$ and $z$ exist for every $x\in\Dcal$.
        \item $B$ is maximal monotone.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
        The first claim is immediate. The second follows from \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents} and manipulating the definition of $B$:
\begin{align*}
    (I+\gamma B)^{-1}x = w &\iff x \in (I+\gamma B)w \\
    &\iff x \in (I+\gamma B)w, \ w = \Ccal^* v \text{ for some } v \text{ with } \Ccal^*v \in \operatorname{dom}B \\
    &\iff x = \Ccal^* v + \gamma z, \ \Ccal z \in \Acal v, \ w = \Ccal^* v.
\end{align*}
For existence, fix $x\in\Dcal$ and, since $\Ccal^*$ is surjective, we can write $x = \Ccal^*\tilde x$.
By \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents} there exists $v$ with $\Ccal\Ccal^*\tilde x \in (\Ccal\Ccal^* + \gamma\Acal)v$, i.e.\ $\gamma^{-1}\Ccal\Ccal^*(\tilde x - v)\in\Acal v$.
Setting $z = \gamma^{-1}\Ccal^*(\tilde x - v)$ gives $\Ccal z\in\Acal v$ and $\Ccal^* v + \gamma z = \Ccal^*\tilde x = x$.

The third item follows from the fact that
\begin{align*}
    \ip{\Ccal^* u_1 - \Ccal^* u_2}{z_1-z_2} = \ip{u_1 - u_2}{\Ccal z_1-\Ccal z_2} \geq 0
\end{align*}
since $\Ccal z_i\in\Acal u_i$ and $\Acal$ is monotone.
Maximality follows from \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents}: by item 2, $\operatorname{range}(I+\gamma B) = \Dcal$, so $B$ is maximal by Minty's theorem \cite[Theorem 21.1]{Bauschke2017FinerPropertiesOfMonotoneOperators}.
\end{proof}

\section{The Degenerate Preconditioned Proximal Point Method}

The idea behind \cite[Theorem 1]{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting} is that we would like to compute the resolvent $J = (I+\gamma B)^{-1}$, but this may be difficult.
Instead, we define a relative error criterion such that the computed iterates lie in the graph of $B$ and satisfy error bounds on how far from the true update they can be (\Cref{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities}).

To do this, we consider a modified version of the algorithm of \cite{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting} (\Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}), where we introduce a stopping rule for the inner loop.
The stopping rule is critical for the analysis of stopping times, and \Cref{prop:certificate} shows that this does not impede convergence of the algorithm.



\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Modified version of the preconditioned HPE algorithm from~\cite{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting}.}\label{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} $w_0 \in \mathcal{D}$, inexactness $\sigma \in [0,1)$, stepsize $\gamma > 0$, inner epoch size $b$, and stopping tolerance $\delta$
\FOR{$k = 0, 1, 2 \ldots$}
  \STATE Produce a pair $(u_{k}, z_{k}) \in \mathcal{H} \times \mathcal{D}$ with
  \[
    \Ccal z_{k} \in \Acal u_{k}, \qquad
    \gamma z_{k} + \Ccal^* u_{k} \approx w_k.
  \]
    \IF {$\|z_{k}\| \leq \delta$}
        \STATE Stop and return $(z_{k}, u_{k}, w_{k})$.
    \ELSIF {$\|\gamma z_{k} + \Ccal^* u_{k} - w_k\| \leq \sigma \|\Ccal_* u_{k} - w_k\|$}
        \STATE Set $w_{k+1} = w_k - \gamma z_{k}$.
    \ELSE
        \STATE Improve the pair $(u_{k}, z_{k}) \in \mathcal{H} \times \mathcal{D}$ with $\Ccal z_{k} \in \Acal u_{k}$ via the iterative procedure $b$ times and go to line 4.
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The following proposition shows that at stopping, the returned iterates provide a certificate of distance to optimality.
Equivalents of the results in this section can be found in \cite{rockafellar1976monotoneoperatorsandppm,bredies2021degeneratepreconditionedproximalpoint,alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting} (among others), but we present them again here for clarity.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:certificate}
    Every output from the $\norm{z}\leq\delta$ terminal test of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} satisfies
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:original-certificate}
    \Ccal z\in\Acal u,\qquad
    \dist(0,\Acal u)\leq\norm{\Ccal z}
    \leq \norm \Ccal\delta.
    \end{equation}
    Recall that the operator $B$ is defined by its graph as
    \begin{equation}
        \gra{B} = \{ (\Ccal^* u, z) : \Ccal z\in\Acal u\}.
    \end{equation}
    and we also defined $J=(I+\gamma B)^{-1}$ and $R=I-J$ (see \Cref{def:b-definition}).
    After stopping we also have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-fp}
    \norm{ \Ccal^* u - J\Ccal^* u}\leq\delta.
    \end{equation}
    That is, the fixed point residual also satisfies the bound at halting.
    Note that the returned iterate may not satisfy the relative error criterion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    The first claim is immediate from the definition of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}.
    For the second, let $q=\Ccal^* u - J\Ccal^* u = (I-J)\Ccal^* u$; then
    \begin{equation*}
        (\Ccal^* u -q,\frac{q}{\gamma}) = (J\Ccal^* u, \frac{q}{\gamma}) = (J\Ccal^* u,\frac{(I-J)\Ccal^* u}{\gamma}).
    \end{equation*}
    Using \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents}, there exists $x$ such that $\Ccal^* x = J \Ccal^*u$.
    Then,
    \begin{align*}
        \Ccal^* x = (I+\gamma B)^{-1}\Ccal^* u &\iff  \Ccal^* u \in \Ccal^*x + \gamma B \Ccal^*x \\
        &\iff  \Ccal^* u = \Ccal^*x + \gamma y, \Ccal y \in \Acal x \\
        &\iff  \frac{\Ccal^* (u - x)}{\gamma} = y, \Ccal y \in \Acal x.
    \end{align*}
    Thus, we can write 
    \begin{align*}
        (J\Ccal^* u,\frac{(I-J)\Ccal^* u}{\gamma}) &= (\Ccal^* x, \gamma^{-1}\Ccal^* (u - x)) ,\quad \frac{\Ccal^* (u - x)}{\gamma} = y, \Ccal y \in \Acal x \\
        &= (\Ccal^* x, y) ,\quad \frac{\Ccal^* (u - x)}{\gamma} = y, \Ccal y \in \Acal x,
    \end{align*}
    so $(\Ccal^* u -q,\frac{q}{\gamma})\in\gra B$.
    Since $(\Ccal^* u, z) \in\gra B$ and $B$ is monotone,
    \begin{align}
        0 \leq \ip{(\Ccal^* u -q) - \Ccal^* u}{\frac{q}{\gamma} - z} = \ip{q}{z-\frac{q}{\gamma}}
    \end{align}
    Thus, $\ip{q}{\gamma z-q}\geq0$ and hence
    \begin{align}
        \norm{\gamma z}^2 = \norm{\gamma z - q}^2 + 2\ip{\gamma z - q}{q} + \norm{q}^2 \geq \norm{q}^2.
    \end{align}
    Applying the stopping rule from \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Outer Convergence Guarantees}\label{sec:outer-convergence-guarantees}
The next lemma is needed to show the convergence of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities}
    The maps $J = (I+\gamma B)^{-1}$ and $R = I - J$ (the fixed point residual) are firmly nonexpansive.
    If $(y,z)\in\gra{B}$ are a candidate update pair (as in line 3 of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} with $y = \Ccal^* u$) at $w$, then
    \begin{align}
        y &= J(\gamma z + y)\label{eq:resolvent-identity-y} \\
        \gamma z &= R(\gamma z + y) \label{eq:fixed-point-identity-z}\\
        \norm{y-J(w)}^2 + \norm{\gamma z-R(w)}^2 &\leq \norm{\gamma z + y - w}^2. \label{eq:graph-error-component-bound}
    \end{align}
    If $(y,z)$ pass the relative test, then we also have
    \begin{align}
        (1-\sigma)\norm{y-w} \leq &\norm{R(w)} \leq (1+\sigma)\norm{y-w} \label{eq:fixed-point-resolvent-bound} \\
        (1-\sigma)\norm{y-w} \leq &\norm{\gamma z} \leq (1+\sigma)\norm{y-w} \label{eq:fixed-point-update-bound}\\
        \norm{\gamma z + y - w} &\leq \frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w)} \label{eq:fixed-point-vs-error-bound}\\
        \norm{\gamma z} &\leq \frac{\norm{R(w)}}{1-\sigma}.\label{eq:fixed-point-update-vs-resolvent-bound}
    \end{align}
    Moreover, $\norm{R(w)} \leq \dist{(w,\zer B)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For the first equation, note that $y = \Ccal^*u$ and thus
    \begin{align}
        y &= J(\gamma z + y) \iff \gamma z + y \in y + \gamma B y \iff z \in B\Ccal^* u.
    \end{align}
    which is the definition of $B$.
    Next,
    \begin{align}
        R(\gamma z + y) = \gamma z + y - J(\gamma z + y) = \gamma z + y - y = \gamma z.
    \end{align}
    Firm nonexpansiveness follows from \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents} and the firm nonexpansiveness of resolvents of maximal monotone operators (see \cite[Corollary 23.11]{BauschkeConvexAnalysisResolventsOfMonotoneOperators}).
    
    Next, by the monotonicity of $B$,
    \begin{align}
        \ip{y_1-y_2}{z_1-z_2} \geq 0,
    \end{align}
    so $\ip{y_1-y_2}{\gamma z_1-\gamma z_2} \geq 0$ as well.
    For an exact pair, $w = y+\gamma z$ with $z \in By$,
    \begin{align}
        \norm{w_1-w_2}^2 &= \norm{\gamma z_1-\gamma z_2}^2 + \norm{y_1-y_2}^2 + 2\ip{y_1-y_2}{\gamma z_1-\gamma z_2} \\
        &\geq \norm{\gamma z_1-\gamma z_2}^2 + \norm{y_1-y_2}^2. \label{eq:component-bound-expansiveness}
    \end{align}
    Note that since $w = y+\gamma z$ with $z \in By$, we have $y = J(w)$ and $\gamma z = R(w)$.
    That is, \Cref{eq:component-bound-expansiveness} states $\norm{w_1-w_2}^2 \geq \norm{R(w_1)-R(w_2)}^2 + \norm{J(w_1)-J(w_2)}^2$ for all $w_1,w_2$.

    Let $e = \gamma z + y -w$, the graph residual.
    Note that $w + e = \gamma z + y$.
    Since $y = J(\gamma z + y) = J(w+e)$, we see that
    \begin{equation}
        \gamma z = w + e - y = w + e - J(w+e) = (I-J)(w+e) = R(w+e).
    \end{equation}
    Thus, using \Cref{eq:resolvent-identity-y,eq:fixed-point-identity-z,eq:component-bound-expansiveness} with $w_1 = w+e$ and $w_2 = w$,
    \begin{align}
        \norm{e}^2 = \norm{(w+e)-w}^2 &\geq \norm{R(w+e)-R(w)}^2 + \norm{J(w+e)-J(w)}^2 \\
        &= \norm{R(\gamma z + y)-R(w)}^2 + \norm{J(\gamma z + y)-J(w)}^2 \\
        &= \norm{\gamma z-R(w)}^2 + \norm{y-J(w)}^2.
    \end{align}

    Now suppose the relative test $\norm{e}\leq\sigma\norm{y-w}$ holds.
    By the reverse triangle inequality,
    \begin{align}
        |\norm{y-w} - \norm{R(w)}| = |\norm{y-w} - \norm{w - J(w)}| \leq \norm{y-J(w)} \leq \norm{e} \leq \sigma\norm{y-w}.
    \end{align}
    This is \Cref{eq:fixed-point-resolvent-bound}.
    Next, $\gamma z = e - (y - w)$, so the triangle inequality gives
    \begin{align*}
        (1-\sigma)\norm{y-w} \leq \norm{y-w} - \norm{e} \leq \norm{\gamma z} \leq \norm{y-w} + \norm{e} \leq (1+\sigma)\norm{y-w},
    \end{align*}
    which is \Cref{eq:fixed-point-update-bound}.
    Combining $\norm{e}\leq\sigma\norm{y-w}$ with the left inequality of \Cref{eq:fixed-point-resolvent-bound} gives \Cref{eq:fixed-point-vs-error-bound}.
    Finally, by \Cref{eq:graph-error-component-bound,eq:fixed-point-vs-error-bound},
    \begin{align*}
        \norm{\gamma z} \leq \norm{R(w)} + \norm{\gamma z - R(w)} \leq \norm{R(w)} + \norm{e} \leq \left(1 + \frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}\right)\norm{R(w)} = \frac{\norm{R(w)}}{1-\sigma},
    \end{align*}
    which is \Cref{eq:fixed-point-update-vs-resolvent-bound}.

    Lastly, by the nonexpansiveness of $R$, if $s\in\zer B$, then $(I+\gamma B)^{-1}s = s$.
    Thus, $R(s) = 0$, so
    \begin{align}
        \norm{R(w)} = \norm{R(w) - R(s)} \leq \norm{w-s}.
    \end{align}
    Taking infimums shows the desired result.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:one-step-decrease-inequality}
    For an accepted pair with $w^+ = w - \gamma z$ and any $s\in\zer B$, 
    \begin{align}
        \norm{w^+ - s}^2 \leq \norm{w - s}^2 - \norm{y-w}^2 + \norm{\gamma z + y - w}^2.
    \end{align}
    Under the relative error rule, this yields
    \begin{align}
        \norm{w^+ - s}^2 &\leq \norm{w - s}^2 - \frac{\gamma^2(1-\sigma)}{1+\sigma}\norm{z}^2 \\
        \norm{w^+ - s}^2 &\leq \norm{w - s}^2 - \frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\norm{R(w)}^2.
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The claim follows immediately from expanding $w = \gamma z + y - e$:
    \begin{align}
        \norm{w -s}^2 &= \norm{w-\gamma z - s}^2 + 2\ip{w-\gamma z - s}{\gamma z} + \norm{\gamma z}^2 \\
        &= \norm{w-\gamma z - s}^2 + 2\ip{y-e - s}{\gamma z} + \norm{\gamma z}^2 \\
        &= \norm{w-\gamma z - s}^2 + 2\ip{y - s}{\gamma z} - 2\ip{e}{\gamma z} + \norm{\gamma z}^2.
    \end{align}
    Using the monotonicity of $B$ (with $(s,0),(y,z)\in\gra B$) on the first inner product term and then completing the square yields 
    \begin{align}
        \norm{w -s}^2 &\geq \norm{w-\gamma z - s}^2 - \norm{e}^2 + \norm{e - \gamma z}^2 \\
        &=  \norm{w-\gamma z - s}^2 - \norm{\gamma z + y - w}^2 + \norm{w-y}^2.
    \end{align}
    Applying the relative error rule and \cref{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities} yields 
    \begin{align}
        \norm{w-\gamma z - s}^2 &\leq \norm{w -s}^2 + \norm{\gamma z + y - w}^2 - \norm{w-y}^2 \\
        &\leq \norm{w -s}^2 + \sigma^2 \norm{y - w}^2 - \norm{w-y}^2 \\
        &\leq \norm{w -s}^2 - (1 - \sigma^2)\norm{w-y}^2 \\
        &\leq \norm{w -s}^2 - \frac{(1 - \sigma^2)}{(1+\sigma)^2}\norm{\gamma z}^2.
    \end{align}
    The last inequality uses $\norm{\gamma z}\leq(1+\sigma)\norm{y-w}$ from \Cref{eq:fixed-point-update-bound}, and $(1-\sigma^2)/(1+\sigma)^2 = (1-\sigma)/(1+\sigma)$, which gives the first bound. Using $\norm{R(w)}\leq(1+\sigma)\norm{y-w}$ from \Cref{eq:fixed-point-resolvent-bound} instead gives the second.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:convergence-guarantee}
    After $m$ accepted updates,
    \begin{align}
        \sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\norm{\gamma z_k}^2 &\leq \frac{(1+\sigma)\dist{(w_0,\zer B)}^2}{1-\sigma}.
    \end{align}
    As a result, 
    \begin{equation}
        \min_{k=0,\dots,m-1}\norm{\Ccal z_k} \leq \frac{\|\Ccal\|\sqrt{1+\sigma}\dist{(w_0,\zer B)}}{\gamma\sqrt{(1-\sigma)m}}.
    \end{equation}
    Moreover, if $K_\delta$ is the number of accepted updates before returning a point satisfying  $\norm{z_{K_\delta}} \leq \delta$ (the terminal test of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}), then
    \begin{align}
        K_{\delta} \leq \left\lfloor \frac{(1+\sigma)\dist{(w_0,\zer B)}^2}{\gamma^2\delta^2(1-\sigma)} \right\rfloor =: K_{\delta}^+.
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    The first two claims follow from the telescoping identity of \Cref{lem:one-step-decrease-inequality} and \Cref{prop:certificate}.
    The last claim follows from the fact that for all iterations before $K_\delta$, the accepted iterate satisfies $\norm{z_k} > \delta$.
    Therefore,
    \begin{align}
            \frac{(1+\sigma)\dist{(w_0,\zer B)}^2}{1-\sigma} \geq \sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta-1}\norm{\gamma z_k}^2 &\geq K_\delta \gamma^2\delta^2.
    \end{align}
    Recognizing that $K_\delta$ is an integer and rearranging yields the result.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
    By \Cref{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities}, bounds on the residual of $\norm{\gamma z_k}$ can be converted into bounds on $\norm{R(w)}$.
    For brevity, we omit them here.
\end{remark}

In essence, \Cref{thm:convergence-guarantee} shows how many iterations are required before stopping, and \Cref{prop:certificate} guarantees the accuracy of the iterate at halting.
We now show that under an error bound guarantee, we obtain linear convergence of the method, where the contraction factor is attenuated by the inexactness of the method.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:error-bound-outer-convergence-guarantee}
    Under an error bound guarantee of the form 
    \begin{align}
        \dist(w_k,\zer B) \leq \kappa \norm{R(w_k)}
    \end{align}
    for all $k$, where $\kappa \geq 1$, we find that
    \begin{align}
        \dist(w_k,\zer B)^2 &\leq \left( 1 - \frac{1-\sigma}{\kappa^2(1+\sigma)} \right)^k \dist(w_0,\zer B)^2 \\
        K_\delta &\leq \frac{\log\left[ \dist(w_0,\zer B)^2 \right] - \log\left[ (1-\sigma)^2\gamma^2\delta^2 \right]}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}},
    \end{align}
    where $\rho = 1 - \frac{1-\sigma}{\kappa^2(1+\sigma)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    From \Cref{lem:one-step-decrease-inequality}, we see that 
    \begin{align}
        \dist(w^+,\zer B)^2 &\leq \dist(w,\zer B)^2 - \frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\norm{R(w)}^2 \\
        &\leq \left( 1 - \frac{1-\sigma}{\kappa^2(1+\sigma)} \right)\dist(w,\zer B)^2.
    \end{align}
    Unraveling the recursion completes the proof of the first inequality.
    
    Next, by \Cref{eq:fixed-point-update-vs-resolvent-bound} and \Cref{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities}. $\norm{z_k}\leq \frac{\dist(w_k,\zer B)}{\gamma(1-\sigma)}$.
    Thus, a sufficient condition for stopping is $\dist(w_k,\zer B)\leq (1-\sigma)\gamma\delta$, which happens after at most 
    \begin{align}
        \frac{\log\left[ \dist(w_0,\zer B)^2 \right] - \log\left[ (1-\sigma)^2\gamma^2\delta^2 \right]}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}}
    \end{align}
    accepted updates, with  $\rho = 1 - \frac{1-\sigma}{\kappa^2(1+\sigma)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    The convergence results of \Cref{thm:convergence-guarantee,thm:error-bound-outer-convergence-guarantee} can be modified to cover the case of deterministic time-varying stepsizes as well.
    This case is a very simple application of \Cref{lem:one-step-decrease-inequality}, and we omit it here.
\end{remark}

\section{Inner Complexity Properties}
However, the analysis of \Cref{sec:outer-convergence-guarantees} ignores the fact that solving the proximal subproblem is usually not trivial.
For example, the proximal operation on the quadratic function $f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^\top A x - b^\top x$ requires solving the system $(\gamma A + I) x^+ = x_0+\gamma b$.
This can be nontrivial in general, and it would be ideal to obtain guarantees for the complexity of running in terms of the number of inner iterations, as well as to connect this to the general structure of the problem and parameters.

We begin with some definitions to establish the setting of the problem.
\begin{definition}
    Let $\prob_k(A) := \prob(A \mid \Fcal_k)$, and $\mathbb{E}_k$ similarly.
    $\Fcal_k$ represents the $\sigma$-field generated by the randomness of the algorithm up to the start of \emph{outer} iteration $k$, i.e.\ all inner and outer history before the $k$th inner solve begins.
    In particular $w_k$, $R(w_k)$, the warm start of the $k$th inner solve, and the event $\{K_\delta \geq k\}$ are $\Fcal_k$-measurable.
    We write $N_k$ for the number of inner iterations performed during outer iteration $k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}\label{assumption:inner-work-contraction}
    We assume that the inner procedure referred to in \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} has a guarantee of the form
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}_k[\norm{\gamma z^l + y^l - w_k}^2] \leq C_0 \alpha^l \mathbb{E}_k[{\norm{\gamma z^0 + y^0 - w_k}^2}]
    \end{align}
    where $\alpha < 1$ and $C_0\geq 1$ is a constant that does not depend not on $k$ or $l$.
    Here the superscripts represent \emph{inner} iterations (those in the loop of lines 4--10 in \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}).
\end{assumption}
This may seem like a strong assumption: we have a method which solves the subproblem linearly.
However, recall the proximal operator scenario.
In this case, the proximal subproblem is strongly convex, and thus gradient descent converges linearly on the regularized subproblem.
In fact, a method which does \emph{not} converge linearly on a strongly convex subproblem is most likely not a good choice of method.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:stopping-time-concentration}
    Under \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents,assumption:inner-work-contraction}, if $\sigma\in(0,1)$, we have the following bounds on the conditional expectation of the inner work of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} at outer iteration $k$, for every integer $l\geq0$:
    \begin{align}
        \prob_k(N_k > bl ) &\leq \min\{1,\beta_k\alpha^{bl}\} \\
        \mathbb{E}_k[N_k] &\leq b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + \frac{b}{1-\alpha^b}
    \end{align}
    where
    \begin{align}
        \beta_k = \frac{(1+\sigma)^2C_0 \mathbb{E}_k[{\norm{\gamma z^0 + y^0 - w_k}^2}]}{\sigma^2\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}^2, \delta^2\}}.
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    On the event $\{N_k > bl\}$, both tests in \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} fail at the check after $bl$ inner iterations.
    Write $y = \Ccal^*u^{bl}$ and $e = \gamma z^{bl} + y - w^k$.
    Thus, $\|\gamma z^{bl} + \Ccal^* u^{bl} - w^k\| > \sigma \|\Ccal^* u^{bl} - w^k\|$.
    % The bounds \Cref{eq:fixed-point-resolvent-bound,eq:fixed-point-update-bound} cannot be used here, since they assume the relative test \emph{passes}.
    Note that \Cref{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities} gives $\norm{y - J(w^k)}\leq\norm{e}$, so
    \begin{align*}
        \norm{R(w^k)} \leq \norm{y - w^k} + \norm{y - J(w^k)} < \frac{\norm{e}}{\sigma} + \norm{e} = \frac{1+\sigma}{\sigma}\norm{e}.
    \end{align*}
    Similarly, since $\gamma z^{bl} = e - (y - w^k)$ and the terminal test fails,
    \begin{align*}
        \delta < \norm{\gamma z^{bl}} \leq \norm{e} + \norm{y - w^k} < \frac{1+\sigma}{\sigma}\norm{e}.
    \end{align*}

    Thus, by the conditional Markov's inequality,
    \begin{align}
        \prob_k(N_k > bl ) &\leq \prob_k\left(\|\gamma z^{bl} + \Ccal^* u^{bl} - w^k\| > \frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}, \delta\} \right) \\
        &\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_k[\|\gamma z^{bl} + \Ccal^* u^{bl} - w^k\|^2]}{\left(\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}, \delta\}\right)^2} \\
        &\leq \beta_k \alpha^{bl}.
    \end{align}
    The expectation bound holds since $N_k$ takes values $0,b,2b,\dots$ and thus
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}_k[N_k] = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\prob_k(N_k > l) &\leq b\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\min\{1,\beta_k\alpha^{bl}\} \label{eq:sum-bound-stopping-time} \\
        &\leq b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + b\beta_k\sum_{l=\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil}^{\infty}\alpha^{bl} \\
        &= b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + b\beta_k\frac{\alpha^{b\cdot\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil}}{1-\alpha^b} \\
        &\leq b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + \frac{b}{1-\alpha^b}.
    \end{align}
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
    One can also show via an integral argument in \Cref{eq:sum-bound-stopping-time} that $\mathbb{E}_k[N_k] \leq b+\frac{1+\logp\beta_k}{\log (1/\alpha)}$.
    \TODO{show when this bound is tighter (for consideration)}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Warm Start Analysis}
The analysis in \Cref{thm:stopping-time-concentration} shows that the stopping time has exponentially decaying tails and thus is finite almost surely.
However, the structure of the guarantee in \Cref{assumption:inner-work-contraction} implies that the method is sensitive to the choice of initialization.
In fact, this is one of the motivations for using the PPM: we can solve a regularized subproblem at each iteration and warm-start the inner solver with the previous iterate.
The following lemma bounds the drift of the solution due to this choice of warm-starting.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:warm-start-bounds}
    Suppose that the inner solve in \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} is warm-started at the previously accepted solution.
    Let $(u_k,z_k)$ be the pair accepted at outer iteration $k$ (as in \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}), $y_k = \Ccal^*u_k$, and $e_k = \gamma z_k + y_k - w_k$, so that $w_{k+1} = w_k - \gamma z_k$.
    Then, we have the following bounds on the drift of the inner system:
    \begin{align}
        \|\gamma z_k + \Ccal^* u_k - w_{k+1}\| &\leq \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)} \label{eq:warm-start-bound}\\
        \norm{R(w_{k+1})} &\leq \frac{1}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)}. \label{eq:residual-drift-bound}
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    From the update rule of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}, we see that
    \begin{align*}
        \gamma z_k + \Ccal^* u_k - w_{k+1} = \gamma z_k + y_k - (w_{k} - \gamma z_k) = e_k + \gamma z_k.
    \end{align*}
    Applying \Cref{eq:fixed-point-vs-error-bound,eq:fixed-point-update-vs-resolvent-bound} shows that
    \begin{align*}
        \norm{\gamma z_k + \Ccal^* u_k - w_{k+1}} &\leq \norm{e_k} + \norm{\gamma z_k} \\
        &\leq \frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)} + \frac{1}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)} \\
        &= \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)}.
    \end{align*}

    For the second inequality, note that $w_{k+1} = J(w_k) + R(w_k) - \gamma z_k$.
    From \Cref{lem:exact-inexact-graph-pair-inequalities}, we see that $\norm{R(w_k) - \gamma z_k}\leq\norm{e_k}\leq\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)}$.
    Since $R$ is nonexpansive and $\norm{R(J(w_k))} = \norm{J(w_k) - J(J(w_k))}\leq\norm{w_k - J(w_k)}$, we get
    \begin{align}
        \norm{R(w_{k+1})} &= \norm{w_{k+1}-J(w_{k+1})} \\
        &= \norm{J(w_k) + R(w_k) - \gamma z_k-J(w_{k}+ R(w_k) - \gamma z_k)} \\
        &\leq\norm{R(w_k)} + \norm{R(w_k) - \gamma z_k} \\
        &\leq\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_k)}.
    \end{align}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:beta-bound}
    This implies that, conditionally, for $k\geq1$ on the event $\{K_\delta\geq k\}$,
    \begin{align}
        \beta_k = \frac{(1+\sigma)^2C_0 \mathbb{E}_k[{\norm{\gamma z_{k-1} + y_{k-1} - w_{k}}^2}]}{\sigma^2\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}^2, \delta^2\}} &\leq \frac{(1+\sigma)^2 C_0 \left( \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\norm{R(w_{k-1})} \right)^2}{\sigma^2\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}^2, \delta^2\}} \\
        &= \frac{D\norm{R(w_{k-1})}^2}{\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}^2, \delta^2\}},
    \end{align}
    where $D := \frac{(1+\sigma)^4C_0}{\sigma^2(1-\sigma)^2}$.
\end{corollary}

Combining \Cref{thm:stopping-time-concentration,lem:warm-start-bounds} yields a bound on the total inner work done to reach a residual of size at most $\delta$.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:total-inner-work-bound}
    Suppose \Cref{assumption:well-defined-resolvents,assumption:inner-work-contraction} hold, $\sigma\in(0,1)$, and for $k\geq1$ the inner solver is warm-started at the previously accepted pair as in \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds}.
    Let $D = \frac{(1+\sigma)^4C_0}{\sigma^2(1-\sigma)^2}$ as in \Cref{cor:beta-bound}, and let $\beta_0$ be the quantity of \Cref{thm:stopping-time-concentration} for the (cold-started) first inner solve.
    Then
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}N_k \right] &\leq \left(\mathbb{E}[K_{\delta}] + 1\right) \frac{b(2-\alpha^b)}{1-\alpha^b} \notag\\
        &\quad + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\left( \mathbb{E}[K_\delta]\log D + \mathbb{E}[\logp\beta_0] + \mathbb{E}\left[ \log\frac{\max\left\{ \norm{R(w_{0})}^2,\delta^2 \right\}}{\delta^2} \right]\right).
        \label{eq:total-inner-work-bound}
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Let $r_k := \max\{\norm{R(w_k)},\delta\}$, $c_1 := \frac{b(2-\alpha^b)}{1-\alpha^b}$, and
    \begin{align*}
        T_k := b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\beta_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + \frac{b}{1-\alpha^b} \leq c_1 + \frac{\logp\beta_k}{\log(1/\alpha)},
    \end{align*}
    where we used $\lceil t\rceil\leq t+1$ and $1 + \frac{1}{1-\alpha^b} = \frac{2-\alpha^b}{1-\alpha^b}$.
    By \Cref{thm:stopping-time-concentration}, $\mathbb{E}_k[N_k]\leq T_k$ on $\{K_\delta\geq k\}$, and $T_k$ is $\Fcal_k$-measurable.

    Fix $k\geq1$ on $\{K_\delta\geq k\}$.
    By \Cref{cor:beta-bound}, $\beta_k\leq D r_{k-1}^2/r_k^2$.
    By \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds} and the fact that $\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\geq1$, $r_k\leq\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}r_{k-1}$, so
    \begin{align*}
        \log D + \log\frac{r_{k-1}^2}{r_k^2} \geq \log\left(D\frac{(1-\sigma)^2}{(1+\sigma)^2}\right) = \log\frac{(1+\sigma)^2C_0}{\sigma^2} \geq 0,
    \end{align*}
    since $C_0\geq1$ and $(1+\sigma)/\sigma>1$.
    Hence the maximum with $0$ in $\logp$ can be dropped:
    \begin{align*}
        \logp\beta_k \leq \max\left\{0,\log D + \log\frac{r_{k-1}^2}{r_k^2}\right\} = \log D + \log\frac{r_{k-1}^2}{r_k^2}.
    \end{align*}
    Summing and telescoping, for every $K\leq K_\delta$,
    \begin{align}
        \sum_{k=1}^{K}\logp\beta_k \leq K\log D + \log\frac{r_0^2}{r_K^2} \leq K\log D + \log\frac{\max\{\norm{R(w_0)}^2,\delta^2\}}{\delta^2},
        \label{eq:telescoped-beta-bound}
    \end{align}
    using $r_K\geq\delta$ to eliminate the maximum in the denominator.
    Taking $K = K_\delta$ gives the almost sure bound
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}T_k \leq (K_\delta+1)c_1 + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\left(\logp\beta_0 + K_\delta\log D + \log\frac{\max\{\norm{R(w_0)}^2,\delta^2\}}{\delta^2}\right).
    \end{align*}
    By Tonelli's theorem and the tower property,
    \begin{align*}
        \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}N_k\right] = \sum_{k=0}^\infty\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{K_\delta\geq k\}}\mathbb{E}_k[N_k]\right] \leq \sum_{k=0}^\infty\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{K_\delta\geq k\}}T_k\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}T_k\right].
    \end{align*}
    Taking expectations in the almost sure bound gives yields the theorem.
    All quantities are finite because $K_\delta\leq K_\delta^+$ almost surely (\Cref{thm:convergence-guarantee}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    The choice of warm start is flexible. The main ingredient is a bound on the initialization in terms of the previous fixed point residual and a bound on the next fixed-point residual in terms of the current.
    That is, it suffices to find constants $G, H$ such that 
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}_{k+1}[\|\gamma z^0 + \Ccal^* u^0 - w_{k+1}\|^2] &\leq G^2\norm{R(w_k)}^2 \quad \text{(warm-start bound)}\\
        \norm{R(w_{k+1})} &\leq H\norm{R(w_k)} \quad \text{(residual drift bound)}
    \end{align}
    where $H\geq 1$.
    The proof of \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound} then goes through with $D$ replaced by $\max\{(1+\sigma)^2C_0G^2/\sigma^2,\ H^2\}$.
    The maximum with $H^2$ is what allows the $\max\{0,\cdot\}$ in $\logp$ to be dropped.
    \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds} gives $G = H = \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}$, for which the maximum is $D$.
    In fact, the proof of \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound} did not really use any properties of the outer algorithm in which it was implemented.
    The main ingredients of the proof are estimates on the drift of the inner subproblem from iteration to iteration.
    It is an interesting question to see if it is possible to apply this strategy to other well-known algorithms which assume some kind of subproblem oracle, such as ADMM and interior-point methods, where the subproblem is nontrivial.
\end{remark}
\begin{corollary}
    From \Cref{thm:convergence-guarantee}, $K_\delta \leq K_\delta^+ = O(\delta^{-2})$.
    Thus, we can expect a total inner work complexity of $O(b\delta^{-2} + \log(1/\delta))$.
    In fact, substituting the value of $K_\delta^+$ shows that the coefficient of $\delta^{-2}$ is $\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\dist(w_0,\zer B)^2\left(\frac{b(2-\alpha^b)}{1-\alpha^b} + \frac{\log D}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{remark}
    Under the assumptions of \Cref{thm:error-bound-outer-convergence-guarantee}, $\mathbb{E}[K_\delta] = O(\log 1/\delta)$, so the amount of total inner work is $O(\log(1/\delta))$.
    This highlights the advantage of having a bound in terms of $\mathbb{E}[K_\delta]$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A deterministic bound]\label{thm:deterministic-inner-work}
    Suppose we have a deterministic guarantee
    \begin{align}
        \norm{\gamma z^l + y^l - w_k}^2 \leq C_0 \alpha^l {\norm{\gamma z^0 + y^0 - w_k}^2}
    \end{align}
    where the constants are the same as in \Cref{assumption:inner-work-contraction}.
    Let $D = \frac{(1+\sigma)^4C_0}{\sigma^2(1-\sigma)^2}$, as before, and define $\tilde{\beta}_k = \frac{(1+\sigma)^2C_0\norm{\gamma z^0 + y^0 - w_k}^2}{\sigma^2\max\{\norm{R(w_k)}^2,\delta^2\}}$, where $(y^0,z^0)$ is the initial pair of the $k$th inner solve.
    If $\sigma\in(0,1)$ and for $k\geq1$ the inner solver is warm-started as in \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds},
    Then every inner solve terminates, with
    \begin{align}
        N_k \leq b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\tilde{\beta}_k}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil \qquad (0\leq k\leq K_\delta),
        \label{eq:deterministic-per-step}
    \end{align}
    and the total inner work satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}N_k &\leq b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\tilde{\beta}_0}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + K_\delta\left(b + \frac{\log D}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right) + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\log\frac{\max\{\norm{R(w_0)}^2,\delta^2\}}{\delta^2} \notag\\
        &\leq b\left\lceil\frac{\logp\tilde{\beta}_0}{b\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil + K_\delta^+\left(b + \frac{\log D}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right) + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\log\frac{\max\{\norm{R(w_0)}^2,\delta^2\}}{\delta^2}.
        \label{eq:deterministic-total}
    \end{align}
    Compared with \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound}, the per-iteration constant $\frac{b(2-\alpha^b)}{1-\alpha^b}$ improves to $b$, because there is no random tail to sum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Fix an outer iteration $k$ and a graph pair $(y,z)$ with residual $e = \gamma z + y - w_k$.
    The proof of \Cref{thm:stopping-time-concentration} shows that if both tests of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} fail at $(y,z)$, then $\norm{e} > \frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\max\{\norm{R(w_k)},\delta\}$.
    That argument used only \Cref{eq:graph-error-component-bound} and the triangle inequality, so no randomness is involved.
    Hence one of the tests passes as soon as $\norm{e}\leq\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\max\{\norm{R(w_k)},\delta\}$.

    At the check after $bl$ inner iterations, the deterministic guarantee gives
    \begin{align*}
        \norm{\gamma z^{bl} + y^{bl} - w_k}^2 \leq C_0\alpha^{bl}\norm{\gamma z^0 + y^0 - w_k}^2 = \tilde{\beta}_k\alpha^{bl}\left(\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^2\max\{\norm{R(w_k)},\delta\}^2.
    \end{align*}
    By the first paragraph, the inner loop exits at the first check $l$ with $\tilde{\beta}_k\alpha^{bl}\leq1$, and $l \geq \lceil\logp\tilde{\beta}_k/(b\log(1/\alpha))\rceil$ satisfies this.
    This proves \Cref{eq:deterministic-per-step}, and in particular $N_k\leq b + \logp\tilde{\beta}_k/\log(1/\alpha)$.

    For $1\leq k\leq K_\delta$, \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds,cor:beta-bound} one can see that the inequalities also hold determinstically.
    The argument in the proof of \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound} therefore gives the analogue of \Cref{eq:telescoped-beta-bound} with $K = K_\delta$:
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_{k=1}^{K_\delta}N_k &\leq K_\delta b + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{K_\delta}\logp\tilde{\beta}_k \\
        &\leq K_\delta\left(b + \frac{\log D}{\log(1/\alpha)}\right) + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\log\frac{\max\{\norm{R(w_0)}^2,\delta^2\}}{\delta^2}.
    \end{align*}
    Adding the bound \Cref{eq:deterministic-per-step} for $k=0$ gives the first inequality in \Cref{eq:deterministic-total}.
    The second follows from $K_\delta\leq K_\delta^+$ (\Cref{thm:convergence-guarantee}).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[An Infinite Inner Loop]
\label{ex:infinite-inner-loop}
Suppose $\Hcal=\Dcal=\R$, $\Ccal=1$, $\gamma=1$, and $\Acal=I$.
This is equivalent to minimizing $f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^2$.
A valid graph pair has $z=u=y$ and error $e=z+y-w=2u-w$.
At the exact outer fixed point $w=0$, take $u_l=z_l=\alpha^{l/2}$ for $0<\alpha<1$, so $u_0 = 1$. 
Then $|e_l|^2=4\alpha^l=\alpha^l|e_0|^2$, but
\[ |e_l|=2|u_l|>\sigma|u_l-w| \qquad(0<\sigma<1) \]
at every finite index.
Consequently a purely relative inner loop never terminates, even under deterministic geometric contraction.
The verified terminal condition $|z_l|=\alpha^{l/2}\leq\delta$ does terminate for every $\delta>0$.  This shows why merely declaring the outer point to be a fixed point, or invoking convergence of $e_l$ to zero, does not justify finite inner termination.
\end{example}

\subsection{Selection of Parameters}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:check-frequency-b-star}
    Suppose the cost of testing is $c_{\text{test}}$, the cost of each inner iteration is $c_{in}$, the cost of each outer iteration is $c_{out}$, and the cost of initialization is $c_{init}$.
    Given an upper bound $K_{\text{up}} \geq K_\delta$, under the assumptions of \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound}, we can bound the total cost $M$ as
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}[M] \leq c_{init} + c_{out}(K_{up}+1) + c_{in}N_{tot} + c_{test}(K_{up} + 1 + b^{-1}N_{tot}),
    \end{align}
    where
    \begin{align}
        N_{tot} &:= (K_{\text{up}}+1)\,b\left(1 + \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right) + N', \\
        N' &:= \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\left(K_{\text{up}}\log D + \mathbb{E}[\logp\beta_0] + \log\frac{\max\left\{ \norm{R(w_{0})}^2,\delta^2 \right\}}{\delta^2}\right),
    \end{align}
    and $D$, $\beta_0$ are as in \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound}.
    The choice of $b\in\mathbb{N}$ that minimizes this upper bound is one of $\lfloor b^\star\rfloor$ or $\lceil b^\star\rceil$, where
    \begin{equation}
        b^\star := \sqrt{\frac{c_{test}\,N'}{c_{in}(K_{up}+1)\left(1 + \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right)}}.
    \end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Since $\frac{2-\alpha^b}{1-\alpha^b} = 1 + \frac{1}{1-\alpha^b}\leq 1 + \frac{1}{1-\alpha}$ for $b\geq1$ and $\mathbb{E}[K_\delta]\leq K_{up}$, \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound} gives $\mathbb{E}[\sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}N_k]\leq N_{tot}$.
    Outer iteration $k$ performs one test before any inner iteration and one after every $b$ inner iterations, i.e.\ $1 + N_k/b$ tests.
    There are $K_\delta + 1$ outer iterations, so
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}[M] &= \mathbb{E}\left[ c_{init} + \sum_{k=0}^{K_\delta}\left[ c_{out} + c_{in}N_k + c_{test}(1 + b^{-1}N_k) \right] \right] \notag\\
        &\leq c_{init} + c_{out}(K_{up}+1) + c_{in}N_{tot} + c_{test}(K_{up} + 1 + b^{-1}N_{tot}) \notag\\
        &= M' + c_{in}(K_{\text{up}}+1)\left(1 + \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right)b + c_{test}N' b^{-1}, \label{eq:check-frequency-upper-bound-looseness}
    \end{align}
    where $M'$ suppresses the terms not involving $b$.
    The right-hand side has the form $f(b) = A + Bb + Cb^{-1}$ with $A,B,C \geq 0$.
    This function is convex on $(0, \infty)$ and minimized at $b^\star = \sqrt{C/B}$.
    Since $f$ is decreasing on $(0, b^\star]$ and increasing on $[b^\star, \infty)$, its minimizer over the positive integers is one of $\lfloor b^\star\rfloor$ or $\lceil b^\star\rceil$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
    This bound is very loose, especially as $\alpha\to 1$, so it may not be suitable for all problems.
    Nevertheless, it is a heuristic that one could estimate before launching their algorithm of choice.
    For an exact answer, one would replace $\frac{1}{1-\alpha}$ in \Cref{eq:check-frequency-upper-bound-looseness} with $\frac{1}{1-\alpha^b}$ and attempt to numerically find the minimum.
\end{remark}

\section{Application to First-Order Methods}
In general, the relationship between parameters is complex and depends on a variety of factors.
Moreover, each choice of algorithm requires a separate analysis of how to optimally choose the parameters $\sigma, b$,  and $\gamma$.
In this section, we consider a few common methods and propose heuristics for selecting parameters.
Note that these heuristics are oriented towards reduction of the fixed-point residual, which may not be the metric of interest in all cases.
Nevertheless, we hope this provides a more principled heuristic for implementations of these algorithms.

\subsection{Proximal Point Method}
As a prescriptive example, let us consider the case of inexact proximal point on a smooth convex function.
The optimal choice of fixed stepsize is $\gamma_{GD} = \frac{2}{(L + 1/\gamma) + 1/\gamma} = \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma L + 2}$, where $\gamma_{GD}$ is the inner stepsize of the gradient method applied to the proximal subproblem $\min_x f(x) + \frac{1}{2\gamma}\norm{x - w}^2$, which is $(L+1/\gamma)$-smooth and $(1/\gamma)$-strongly convex.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:optimal-sigma-gd}
    Consider the case where $\Ccal = I$ and $\Acal = \nabla f$ where $f$ is convex and $L$-smooth.
    At outer iteration $k$, run vanilla gradient descent with stepsize $\gamma_{GD} = \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma L + 2}$ on the proximal subproblem $\argmin_{x}\varphi_k(x) := f(x) + \frac{1}{2\gamma}\norm{x - w_k}^2$, warm-started at the previously accepted $u$, and use the graph pair $(u^l, z^l) = (x^l, \nabla f(x^l))$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The deterministic guarantee of \Cref{thm:deterministic-inner-work}, and hence \Cref{assumption:inner-work-contraction}, holds with
        \begin{align}
            \alpha = \left(\frac{\gamma L}{\gamma L + 2}\right)^2, \qquad C_0 = (1+\gamma L)^2.
        \end{align}
        \item Let $c := \frac{b(2-\alpha^b)\log(1/\alpha)}{1-\alpha^b}$ for the bound of \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound}, or $c := b\log(1/\alpha)$ for the bound of \Cref{thm:deterministic-inner-work}.
        Substitute $\mathbb{E}[K_\delta]\leq K_\delta^+\leq\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\cdot\frac{\dist(w_0,\zer B)^2}{\gamma^2\delta^2}$ into either bound.
        The coefficient of $\delta^{-2}$ is then $\frac{\dist(w_0,\zer B)^2}{\log(1/\alpha)}\,g(\sigma)$, where
        \begin{align}
            g(\sigma) := \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\bigl(c + \log D(\sigma)\bigr), \qquad D(\sigma) = \frac{(1+\sigma)^4C_0}{\sigma^2(1-\sigma)^2}.
        \end{align}
        Over $\sigma\in(0,1)$, $g$ has a unique minimizer $\sigma^\star$, which is the unique solution in $(0,1)$ of
        \begin{align}
            \frac{1}{\sigma} = 3 + c + \log\frac{(1+\sigma)^4C_0}{\sigma^2(1-\sigma)^2}.
            \label{eq:optimal-sigma}
        \end{align}
        Moreover,
        \begin{align}
            \sigma^\star < \frac{1}{3 + c + \log C_0} = \frac{1}{3 + c + 2\log(1+\gamma L)}.
        \end{align}
    \end{enumerate}
    The other $\sigma$-dependent term in the bounds, $\logp\beta_0/\log(1/\alpha)$, does not grow as $\delta\to0$.
    So $\sigma^\star$ minimizes the upper bound to leading order in $\delta^{-2}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Here $B = \nabla f$, $y = u$, and $J = (I+\gamma\nabla f)^{-1}$ maps $w_k$ to the minimizer $x_k^\star$ of $\varphi_k$.
    The pair $(x^l,\nabla f(x^l))$ lies in $\gra B$, and its residual is $e^l = \gamma\nabla f(x^l) + x^l - w_k = \gamma\nabla\varphi_k(x^l)$.
    The function $\varphi_k$ is $\mu$-strongly convex and $L_\varphi$-smooth with $\mu = 1/\gamma$ and $L_\varphi = L + 1/\gamma$, so its condition number is $L_\varphi/\mu = 1 + \gamma L$.
    With stepsize $2/(L_\varphi+\mu) = \gamma_{GD}$, gradient descent satisfies $\norm{x^l - x_k^\star}\leq q^l\norm{x^0 - x_k^\star}$ with $q = \frac{L_\varphi - \mu}{L_\varphi + \mu} = \frac{\gamma L}{\gamma L + 2}$~\cite[Theorem 2.1.15]{nesterov2004introductory}.
    Using $\mu\norm{x - x_k^\star}\leq\norm{\nabla\varphi_k(x)}\leq L_\varphi\norm{x - x_k^\star}$,
    \begin{align*}
        \norm{e^l}^2 = \gamma^2\norm{\nabla\varphi_k(x^l)}^2 \leq \gamma^2L_\varphi^2q^{2l}\norm{x^0 - x_k^\star}^2 \leq \left(\frac{L_\varphi}{\mu}\right)^2q^{2l}\gamma^2\norm{\nabla\varphi_k(x^0)}^2 = (1+\gamma L)^2q^{2l}\norm{e^0}^2.
    \end{align*}
    This holds deterministically for every $l$ and $k$.

    Define $S = \frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}$ and $\Delta = \dist(w_0,\zer B)^2/(\gamma^2\delta^2)$.
    The bound of \Cref{thm:total-inner-work-bound} is at most
    \begin{align*}
        \frac{S\Delta}{\log(1/\alpha)}\bigl(c + \log D(\sigma)\bigr) + \frac{b(2-\alpha^b)}{1-\alpha^b} + \frac{1}{\log(1/\alpha)}\left(\logp\beta_0 + \log\frac{\max\{\norm{R(w_0)}^2,\delta^2\}}{\delta^2}\right),
    \end{align*}
    The bound of \Cref{thm:deterministic-inner-work} has the same $\delta^{-2}$ term with $c = b\log(1/\alpha)$, and its remaining terms also do not grow with $\delta^{-1}$ faster than $\log(1/\delta)$.
    This identifies the $\delta^{-2}$ coefficient as claimed.
    Here $\alpha$, $C_0$, and hence $c$ do not depend on $\sigma$.
    Using $\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log S = \frac{2}{1-\sigma^2}$ and $\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log D = \frac{4}{1+\sigma} - \frac{2}{\sigma} + \frac{2}{1-\sigma}$, we get $\frac{1-\sigma^2}{2}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log D = 3 - \frac{1}{\sigma}$, and hence
    \begin{align*}
        g'(\sigma) &= \frac{2S}{1-\sigma^2}\left(c + \log D(\sigma) + 3 - \frac{1}{\sigma}\right) \\
        &= -\frac{2S}{1-\sigma^2} h(\sigma) \\
        h(\sigma) &:= \frac{1}{\sigma} - 3 - c - \log D(\sigma).
    \end{align*}
    Now $h(\sigma)\to+\infty$ as $\sigma\downarrow0$, since $1/\sigma$ dominates $\log D(\sigma) = 2\log(1/\sigma) + O(1)$.
    Also $h(\sigma)\to-\infty$ as $\sigma\uparrow1$, since $\log D(\sigma)\to\infty$.
    Moreover, $h$ is strictly decreasing:
    \begin{align*}
        h'(\sigma) = \left(-\frac{1}{\sigma^2} + \frac{2}{\sigma}\right) - \frac{4}{1+\sigma} - \frac{2}{1-\sigma} \leq 1 - 2 - 2 < 0,
    \end{align*}
    since $\frac{2}{1-\sigma}>2$ for $\sigma\in(0,1)$.
    Hence $h$ has a unique root $\sigma^\star$, which is the solution to \Cref{eq:optimal-sigma}.
    Furthermore $g'<0$ on $(0,\sigma^\star)$ and $g'>0$ on $(\sigma^\star,1)$, so $\sigma^\star$ is the unique minimizer.
    Finally, $\log D(\sigma) = \log C_0 + 4\log(1+\sigma) + 2\log\frac{1}{\sigma} + 2\log\frac{1}{1-\sigma} > \log C_0$ on $(0,1)$.
    Substituting into \Cref{eq:optimal-sigma} gives $1/\sigma^\star > 3 + c + \log C_0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
    Equation \Cref{eq:optimal-sigma} is easy to solve by bisection, since $h$ is monotone.
    As a sanity check (not a result), we solved it numerically for $\gamma L\in\{0.1,1,10,100\}$ and $b\in\{1,5\}$, for both choices of $c$.
    The root agreed with a brute-force grid minimization of $g$ to six digits, and $\sigma^\star$ ranged between about $0.014$ and $0.083$.
    So this upper bound favours fairly accurate inner solves.
    Because the analysis is worst-case, this should be treated only as a heuristic.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Douglas Rachford}
\cite{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting} shows that the inexact Douglas-Rachford splitting of \cite{EcksteinRelativeError2018} fits into their framework.
We reproduce the formulation here.
Suppose we wish to solve
\begin{align}
    \text{find } x \text{ s.t. } 0 \in\Acal_1 x + \Acal_2 x, \label{eq:Douglas-Rachford-inclusion}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{A}_1$ and $\mathcal{A}_2$ are maximal monotone operators.
We introduce the operators
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
        \tau\Acal_1 & I & -I \\
        -I & \tau\Acal_2 & I \\
        I & -I & 0 \\
    \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \Ccal = \begin{bmatrix}
        I \\ -I \\ I
    \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathcal{M} = \Ccal\Ccal^*,
\end{align}
and set $\gamma = 1$.
One can check that $(\Ccal\Ccal^* + \gamma \Acal)^{-1}\Ccal\Ccal^*$ is defined everywhere.
Note that if $0\in\Acal(x_1,x_2,u)$, then $x_1=x_2$ and $0\in\Acal_1 x + \Acal_2 x$.
Substituting this into \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} yields \Cref{alg:inexact-drs}.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{The method introduced by Eckstein and Yao in~\cite{EcksteinRelativeError2018} (copied from~\cite[Algorithm 2]{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting})}\label{alg:inexact-drs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} $w^0 \in H$ and $\sigma \in [0,1)$
\FOR{$k = 1, 2 \ldots$}
  \STATE Define $(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}, a_1^{k+1})$ such that $a_1^{k+1} \in \Acal_1 \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}$ fulfill
  \[
    \tau a_1^{k+1} + \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} \approx w^k .
  \]
  \STATE Find $(\widetilde{x}_2^{k+1}, a_2^{k+1})$ such that $a_2^{k+1} \in \Acal_2 \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1}$ and
  \[
    \tau a_2^{k+1} + \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} = \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} - \tau a_1^{k+1} .
  \]
  \item[] (We compute $\widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} = J_{\tau \Acal_2}(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} - a_1^{k+1})$ and take
  \item[] $\tau a_2^{k+1} = \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} - \tau a_1^{k+1} - \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1}$.)
  \WHILE{$\|\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} + \tau a_1^{k+1} - w^k\| > \sigma \|\widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} + \tau a_1^{k+1} - w^k\|$}
    \STATE Improve $(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}, a_1^{k+1})$ with $a_1^{k+1} \in \Acal_1 \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}$ to reduce
    \item[] $\|\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} + \tau a_1^{k+1} - w^k\|$.
    \STATE Similar to step 4 find $(\widetilde{x}_2^{k+1}, a_2^{k+1})$ such that $a_2^{k+1} \in \Acal_2 \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1}$ and
    \[
      \tau a_2^{k+1} + \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} = \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} - \tau a_1^{k+1} .
    \]
  \ENDWHILE
  \STATE Update
  \[
    w^{k+1} = w^k - \tau (a_1^{k+1} + a_2^{k+1}),
  \]
  \item[] (or, equivalently, $w^{k+1} = w^k + \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} - \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}$).
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{ADMM}
\cite{EcksteinRelativeError2018} shows how their formulation yields a partially inexact version of ADMM.
Suppose we want to solve
\begin{align}
    \min_{x,z} \quad &f(x) + g(z) \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad &x = z
    \label{eq:admm-problem}
\end{align}
where $f$ and $g$ are closed, proper, and convex functions, but not necessarily smooth.
Its optimality condition is $0\in\partial f(x) + \partial g(x)$, i.e.\ \Cref{eq:Douglas-Rachford-inclusion} with $\Acal_1 = \partial f$ and $\Acal_2 = \partial g$, whenever $\partial(f+g) = \partial f + \partial g$ (e.g.\ if $\operatorname{ri}\operatorname{dom} f\cap\operatorname{ri}\operatorname{dom} g\neq\varnothing$).
\cite{EcksteinRelativeError2018} describe a variant of ADMM which produces iterates as (equations (25)-(27))
\begin{align}
    x_{k+1} &\approx \argmin_{x} f(x) + \ip{p_k}{x} + \frac{c}{2}\norm{x-z_k}^2 \\
    p_{k+1} &= p_k + c(x_{k+1}-z_k) \\
    z_{k+1} &= \argmin_{z} g(z) - \ip{p_{k+1}}{z} + \frac{c}{2}\norm{z-x_{k+1}}^2\added{.}
    \added{\label{eq:admm-iterates}}
\end{align}
We reproduce their algorithm within the framework established by \cite{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting}.
Take $\Acal_1 = \partial f$, $\Acal_2 = \partial g$, and $\tau = 1/c$ in the Douglas--Rachford instance above, that is,
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
        \frac{1}{c}\partial f & I & -I \\
        -I & \frac{1}{c}\partial g & I \\
        I & -I & 0 \\
    \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \Ccal = \begin{bmatrix}
        I \\ -I \\ I
    \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \gamma = 1,
    \label{eq:admm-operators}
\end{align}
and change variables by $w_k = z_k - p_k/c$.
Since $\Ccal^*(h_1,h_2,h_3) = h_1 - h_2 + h_3$, $\Ccal^*$ is surjective.
Also, $0\in\Acal(x_1,x_2,v)$ if and only if $x_1 = x_2 =: x$ and $v - x\in\frac{1}{c}\partial f(x)$, $x - v\in\frac{1}{c}\partial g(x)$.
Hence $\zer\Acal\neq\varnothing$ if and only if $0\in\partial f(x)+\partial g(x)$ has a solution.


Only the $f$-subproblem is solved inexactly.
The inner solver returns a pair $(x_{k+1}, a_{k+1})$ with $a_{k+1}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})$; for example, $a_{k+1} = \nabla f(x_{k+1})$ if $f$ is differentiable.
The $g$-subproblem is solved exactly.
Writing $\varphi_k(x) := f(x) + \ip{p_k}{x} + \frac{c}{2}\norm{x - z_k}^2$ for the $x$-subproblem, the graph residual of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} becomes
\begin{align}
    x_{k+1} + \frac{a_{k+1}}{c} - w_k = \frac{1}{c}\bigl(a_{k+1} + p_k + c(x_{k+1} - z_k)\bigr) \in \frac{1}{c}\partial\varphi_k(x_{k+1}).
    \label{eq:admm-residual}
\end{align}
That is, it is a scaled subgradient of the $x$-subproblem objective, which is zero exactly when $x_{k+1}$ solves the subproblem with $a_{k+1} = -p_{k+1}$.
Substituting into \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} yields \Cref{alg:inexact-admm}.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Inexact ADMM as an instance of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}.}\label{alg:inexact-admm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} $z_0, p_0$, penalty $c>0$, inexactness $\sigma\in(0,1)$, inner epoch size $b$, stopping tolerance $\delta$, and $x$ (e.g.\ $x = z_0$)
\FOR{$k = 0, 1, 2 \ldots$}
  \STATE Produce $(x_{k+1}, a_{k+1})$ with $a_{k+1}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})$ and $x_{k+1}\approx\argmin_x \varphi_k(x)$, warm-started at $(x_k, a_k)$ for $k\geq1$.
  \STATE Compute $z_{k+1} = \argmin_z g(z) + \ip{a_{k+1}}{z} + \frac{c}{2}\norm{z - x_{k+1}}^2 = \operatorname{prox}_{g/c}\bigl(x_{k+1} - a_{k+1}/c\bigr)$.
    \IF {$\norm{x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}} \leq \delta$}
        \STATE Stop and return $(x_{k+1}, z_{k+1}, a_{k+1})$.
    \ELSIF {$\norm{a_{k+1} + p_k + c(x_{k+1} - z_k)} \leq \sigma \norm{a_{k+1} + p_k + c(z_{k+1} - z_k)}$}
        \STATE Set $p_{k+1} = p_k + c(x_{k+1} - z_k)$ and keep $z_{k+1}$.
    \ELSE
        \STATE Improve $(x_{k+1}, a_{k+1})$ with $a_{k+1}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})$ via the iterative procedure $b$ times and go to line 4.
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When the $x$-subproblem is solved exactly, $a_{k+1} = -p_{k+1}$, and lines 4 and 8 reduce to \Cref{eq:admm-iterates}.
Each check (line 4) costs one evaluation of $\operatorname{prox}_{g/c}$; this is the test cost $c_{test}$ in the parameter selection analysis of \Cref{lem:check-frequency-b-star}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:admm}
    Let $\Acal,\Ccal$ be as in \Cref{eq:admm-operators}, and suppose $w_0 = z_0 - p_0/c$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \Cref{alg:inexact-admm} is \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} applied to $\Acal,\Ccal$ with $\gamma=1$, with $w_k = z_k - p_k/c$ for all $k$. Its terminal and relative tests are those of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm}.
        Hence \Cref{thm:convergence-guarantee,thm:stopping-time-concentration,thm:total-inner-work-bound,thm:deterministic-inner-work} apply, with the warm start of line 3 playing the role of the warm start in \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds}.
        \item At termination, $a_{k+1}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})$ and $\tilde a := c(x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}) - a_{k+1}\in\partial g(z_{k+1})$, with
        \begin{align}
            \norm{x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}}\leq\delta, \qquad \norm{a_{k+1} + \tilde a} = c\norm{x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}} \leq c\delta.
        \end{align}
        That is, $(x_{k+1}, z_{k+1})$ satisfies the optimality conditions of \Cref{eq:admm-problem} up to $\delta$ in primal feasibility and $c\delta$ in dual feasibility.
        \item If $f$ is $L$-smooth and the inner procedure is gradient descent on $\varphi_k$ with stepsize $\frac{2}{L+2c}$ and $a^l = \nabla f(x^l)$, then the deterministic guarantee of \Cref{thm:deterministic-inner-work} holds with
        \begin{align}
            \alpha = \left(\frac{L}{L+2c}\right)^2, \qquad C_0 = \left(1 + \frac{L}{c}\right)^2.
        \end{align}
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    We start with the proof of the first item.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Write the variables of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} as $u = (x_1, x_2, v)$, with $\zeta$ in place of its $z$, to avoid a    clash with the ADMM variable $z$.
            Expanding $\Ccal\zeta\in\Acal u$ row by row, the condition is equivalent to
            \begin{align*}
                a_1 := c(\zeta - x_2 + v)\in\partial f(x_1), \qquad c(x_2 - v)\in\partial g(x_2), \qquad \zeta = x_1 - x_2.
            \end{align*}
            Given $(x_{k+1}, a_{k+1})$ with $a_{k+1}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})$ and $z_{k+1}$ from line 4, set
            \begin{align*}
                x_1 = x_{k+1}, \quad x_2 = z_{k+1}, \quad \zeta = x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}, \quad v = \frac{a_{k+1}}{c} - \zeta + z_{k+1}.
            \end{align*}
            The first and third conditions hold by construction.
            The second reads $c(x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}) - a_{k+1}\in\partial g(z_{k+1})$, which is the optimality condition of the $z$-subproblem in line 4.
            So line 4 produces a graph pair of $B$, and conversely every graph pair arises this way from its first block.
            With $y = \Ccal^*u = x_1 - x_2 + v$ and $\gamma = 1$,
            \begin{align*}
                \gamma\zeta + y - w_k = x_{k+1} + \frac{a_{k+1}}{c} - w_k, \qquad y - w_k = z_{k+1} + \frac{a_{k+1}}{c} - w_k.
            \end{align*}
            Substituting $w_k = z_k - p_k/c$ and multiplying by $c$, the relative test of \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} becomes line 7.
            Its terminal test $\norm{\zeta}\leq\delta$ becomes line 5.
            For the update, $w_{k+1} = w_k - \zeta = w_k - x_{k+1} + z_{k+1}$.
            Defining $p_{k+1} := c(z_{k+1} - w_{k+1})$ preserves $w_{k+1} = z_{k+1} - p_{k+1}/c$ and gives
            \begin{align*}
                p_{k+1} = c(x_{k+1} - w_k) = p_k + c(x_{k+1} - z_k),
            \end{align*}
            which is line 8.
            Finally, $(x_1,x_2,v,\zeta)$ is a function of $(x_{k+1}, a_{k+1})$ (and $w_k$), so warm-starting the $f$-solver at $(x_k, a_k)$ reproduces the previously accepted graph pair, as required in \Cref{lem:warm-start-bounds}.
            Note also that the residual \Cref{eq:admm-residual} depends only on the $f$-solver's output, so the contraction assumptions concern the $f$-subproblem alone.
        
        \item Now, for the second item, $\tilde a\in\partial g(z_{k+1})$ is the second graph condition above, and $a_{k+1}+\tilde a = c(x_{k+1} - z_{k+1}) = c\zeta$.
                The bounds follow from the terminal test.
        
        \item Finally, up to an additive constant, $\varphi_k(x) = f(x) + \frac{c}{2}\norm{x - w_k}^2$, so $\varphi_k$ is the proximal subproblem of     \Cref{prop:optimal-sigma-gd} with $\gamma$ replaced by $1/c$.
            By \Cref{eq:admm-residual}, the residual of the gradient descent iterate $x^l$ is $\frac{1}{c}\nabla\varphi_k(x^l)$, which matches the residual $\gamma\nabla\varphi_k(x^l)$ in the proof of \Cref{prop:optimal-sigma-gd}.
            The stepsize $\frac{2}{L+2c} = \frac{2(1/c)}{(1/c)L + 2}$ also matches.
            That proof therefore gives the the third item with $\gamma L$ replaced by $L/c$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Inexact Davis-Yin}
We can extend the Douglas-Rachford result to the more general Davis-Yin case.

\begin{align}
    \text{find } x \text{ s.t. } 0 \in\Acal_1 x + \Acal_2 x + \mathcal{B}x, \label{eq:Davis-Yin-inclusion}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{A}_1$ and $\mathcal{A}_2$ are maximal monotone operators and $\mathcal{B}$ is $\beta^{-1}$-cocoercive.
We define 
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
        \alpha\Acal_1 & I & -I \\
        -I & \tau\Acal_2 & I \\
        I & -I & 0 \\
    \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \Ccal = \begin{bmatrix}
        I \\ -I \\ I
    \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathcal{M} = \Ccal\Ccal^*,
\end{align}

Substituting this into \Cref{alg:stopping-time-algorithm} yields \Cref{alg:inexact-davis-yin}.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Inexact version of the Davis-Yin algorithm.}\label{alg:inexact-davis-yin}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} $w^0 \in H$ and $\sigma \in [0,1)$, $\gamma \in \left(0, \frac{2}{\beta}\right)$, $\alpha = \frac{\gamma\beta}{4-\gamma\beta}$.
\FOR{$k = 1, 2 \ldots$}
  \STATE First define $(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}, a_1^{k+1})$ such that $a_1^{k+1} \in A_1 \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}$ and
  \[
    \gamma a_1^{k+1} + \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} \approx \widetilde{w}^k
  \]
  \STATE Then take
  \[
    \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} = J_{\gamma A_2}\left(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} - \gamma a_1^{k+1} - \gamma B \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}\right).
  \]
  \WHILE{$\|\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} + \gamma a_1^{k+1} - \widetilde{w}^k\| > \sigma \left\| \frac{\alpha \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} + \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1}}{1+\alpha} + \gamma a_1^{k+1} - \widetilde{w}^k \right\|$}
    \STATE Improve $(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}, a_1^{k+1})$ with $a_1^{k+1} \in A_1 \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}$ to reduce
    \item[] $\|\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} + \gamma a_1^{k+1} - \widetilde{w}^k\|$.
    \STATE Then take
    \[
      \widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} = J_{\gamma A_2}\left(\widetilde{x}_1^{k+1} - \gamma a_1^{k+1} - \gamma B \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}\right).
    \]
  \ENDWHILE
  \STATE Update
  \[
    \widetilde{w}^{k+1} = \widetilde{w}^k + \frac{1}{1+\alpha}\left(\widetilde{x}_2^{k+1} - \widetilde{x}_1^{k+1}\right).
  \]
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
