\section{Introduction}
I think the focus should be on how we have a very general framework for analyzing first-order methods which require a subproblem oracle.
This framework is plug-and-play: someone can take their favorite first-order method, choose a particular inner solver, and substitute the convergence guarantees for that inner solver into our work to get heuristics for parameter selection.
Theoretically, we improve previous total work bounds from $O(\delta^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ to $O(\delta^{-2})$ by considering the effect of warm-starting, rather than a naive (worst inner) $\times$ (worst outer) bound.
We can also emphasize that we allow randomized inner methods and show some high-probability guarantees for these.
