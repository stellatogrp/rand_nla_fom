\title{Integrated Analysis of an Algorithm for Linearly Constrained Convex Optimization}
\author{Pranav Reddy}
\date{\small \today \vspace{-5mm}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
    We consider the minimization of a closed proper convex function subject to linear equality constraints.
    We propose a relaxed Douglas--Rachford method in which the proximal step is exact and the linear system that forms the other step is solved inexactly, by any iterative method, up to a relative-error test on its residual.
    We make no smoothness or growth assumptions on the objective.
    We show that the squared fixed-point residual is summable, so that its running minimum decays at an asymptotiic rate of $o(1/k)$, and that the iterates converge linearly when the objective is smooth and strongly convex.
    We then give an integrated analysis of the inner work: for a linear solver that contracts the residual geometrically, the expected number of inner iterations per outer iteration is bounded independently of the iteration count, and the total inner work over $n$ outer iterations is bounded by a constant times $n$ plus a logarithmic term.
    The analysis rests on an explicit description of the spectrum of the linear system in terms of the step sizes and the singular values of the constraint matrix.
    Numerical experiments on linear programs, quadratic programs, and the LASSO confirm the analysis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Consider the problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearly-constrained-problem}
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \text{minimize} & f(x)\\
        \text{subject to} & Ax = b,
    \end{array}
\end{equation}
where $x \in \R^n$ is the optimization variable, $A \in \R^{m\times n}$ and $b\in\R^m$ are problem data, and $f$ is convex, closed, and proper, but not necessarily differentiable.
A popular class of methods for solving this problem is operator splitting, which decomposes \Cref{eq:linearly-constrained-problem} into smaller subproblems and solves them in turn.
For an introduction to the theory of monotone operators and their application in these methods, see \citet{Ryu_Yin_OptimizationMonotoneOperators}.
The hope is that by splitting, a previously difficult problem may be decomposed into a sequence of simple operations that are easier to implement.
This broad idea finds many applications in convex optimization, with notable success stories such as ISTA, FISTA, and PDLP and its variants \citep{ISTA-original-paper,FISTA-original-paper,applegate2022practicallargescalelinearprogramming-pdlp-paper}.

However, splitting alone does not always guarantee simplicity.
Proximal subproblems, such as projections onto convex sets, often have no closed-form solution and require iterative subroutines of their own.
Since solving them accurately is difficult and largely unnecessary in the early stages of an algorithm, the optimization community has had a sustained interest in inexact algorithms for over 40 years \citep{rockafellar-ppm-paper,EcksteinBertsekasDouglasRachfordSplitting,svaiter2018weaklyconvergentfullyinexact,alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting,liang2015convergenceratesinexactnonexpansive,schmidt2011convergenceratesinexactproximalgradient,BarrePEPInexactMethods,CortildInexactKMIterations}.

We focus on the problem class \Cref{eq:linearly-constrained-problem}, which is broad enough to include linear and quadratic programming as well as other popular convex problems such as LASSO. %, and on a method in which the proximal step is exact and the linear solve is inexact.
Our contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate a relaxed Douglas--Rachford method for \Cref{eq:linearly-constrained-problem} in a block-diagonal metric with separate step sizes $\gamma_x$ and $\gamma_\lambda$ for the primal and dual variables, respectively, in which a linear system is solved inexactly up to a relative-error test on its residual.
    We show that the test is admissible whenever $\sigma < (2 - \theta)/2$, where $\theta \in (0, 2)$ is the relaxation parameter and $\sigma$ is an inexactness parameter (\Cref{sec:algorithm}).
    \item Without any smoothness or growth assumption on $f$, we show that the squared fixed-point residual is summable and that its running minimum decays as $o(1/k)$.
    Furthermore, when $f$ is smooth and strongly convex, we show linear convergence with an explicit rate (\Cref{sec:algorithm}).
    \item We give an integrated analysis of the inner work.
    For any inner solver that contracts the residual geometrically, the expected number of inner iterations at each outer iteration is bounded by a quantity that depends on the tolerance $\sigma$ and the contraction factor but not on the iteration count, and the total inner work over $n$ outer iterations is bounded by a constant times $n$ plus a logarithm of the progress made (\Cref{sec:linear-system}).
    The bridge between the two levels is an explicit description of the spectrum of the linear system in terms of $\gamma_x$, $\gamma_\lambda$, and the singular values of $A$.
    \item We test the method on linear programs, quadratic programs with equality and inequality constraints, and the LASSO, with conjugate gradients, GMRES, block Kaczmarz, and randomized Kaczmarz as inner solvers (\Cref{sec:numerical-results}).
\end{enumerate}
The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
\Cref{sec:setting} fixes notation and the standing assumptions.
\Cref{sec:algorithm} derives the method and proves its convergence.
\Cref{sec:linear-system} analyzes the inner work and the spectrum of the linear system.
\Cref{sec:numerical-results} reports the numerical experiments, and \Cref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
All code is available at \url{https://github.com/stellatogrp/rand_nla_fom}.


\section{Setting and Notation}\label{sec:setting}
Superscripts in parentheses, as in $x^{(k)}$, denote the \emph{outer} iterations, at which the proximal step is taken.
Subscripts in parentheses, as in $x^{(k)}_{(l)}$, denote the \emph{inner} iterations of the iterative method that solves the linear system at outer iteration $k$.
That is, the inner method is initialized at $x^{(k)}_{(0)}$ and $x^{(k)}_{(l)}$ is its $l$th iterate.

We use $\partial f(x)$ to denote the set of subgradients of $f$ at $x$.
We use $\prox_{f}(x)$ to denote the proximal operator of $f$ evaluated at $x$:
\begin{equation*}
    \prox_{f}(x) = \argmin_{y} \left\{f(y) + \frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^2\right\}.
\end{equation*}
A norm written without a subscript is the standard Euclidean norm, $\|x\|^2 = x^\top x$.
For a positive definite matrix $M$ we write $\|x\|_M^2 = x^\top M x$ and $\langle x, y\rangle_M = x^\top M y$.
The analysis uses only norms of this form. but the numerical section also uses the $\ell_1$ and $\ell_\infty$ norms, which are written with their subscripts.
The positive part of the logarithm is denoted $\logp t = \max\{\log t, 0\}$.

We write $\sigma_1(A) \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r(A) > 0$ for the nonzero singular values of $A$, where $r = \Rank(A)$, and $\sigma_{\max}(A) = \sigma_1(A)$.
The smallest nonzero singular value is $\sigma_{\min}^+(A) = \sigma_r(A)$.
When $A$ has full row rank, $r = m$ and we also write $\sigma_{\min}(A)$ for it.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:standing}
    We make the following four assumptions throughout the paper.
    These are standard and otherwise unremarkable, but we list them here for completeness.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The function $f \colon \R^n \to \R \cup \{+\infty\}$ is closed, proper, and convex.
        \item The matrix $A$ is nonzero.
        \item A KKT point of \Cref{eq:linearly-constrained-problem} exists.
        \item The step sizes satisfy $\gamma_x > 0$ and $\gamma_\lambda > 0$.
    \end{enumerate}
    If a result necessitates a full row rank assumption on $A$, we will mention it.
\end{assumption}


\section{Algorithm}\label{sec:algorithm}
\Cref{alg:main} states the method.
At each outer iteration, we solve \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} approximately until the residual passes a relative-error test $\|\varepsilon\|_M \leq \sigma\|s\|_M$, then takes the relaxed step $z^{(k+1)} = z^{(k)} + \theta s^{(k+1)}$.
Since $x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} + \theta(v^{(k+1)} - \xi^{(k+1)})$ and $\lambda^{(k+1)} = \lambda^{(k)} + \theta\gamma_\lambda(A\xi^{(k+1)} - b)$, the update consists of one proximal step, one product with $A$, and the inner solve.
The primal iterate that is reported is $v^{(k+1)}$, the output of the proximal step.
\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
    \caption{Relaxed Douglas--Rachford with an inexact linear solve}
    \label{alg:main}
    \KwIn{$z^{(0)} = (x^{(0)}, \lambda^{(0)})$; step sizes $\gamma_x, \gamma_\lambda > 0$; relaxation $\theta \in (0, 2)$; tolerance $\sigma \in [0, (2-\theta)/2)$}
    \For{$k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$}{
        find $(\xi^{(k+1)}, \nu^{(k+1)})$, by an iterative method applied to \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} at $z^{(k)}$ and warm started at $(\xi^{(k)}, \nu^{(k)})$, whose step $s^{(k+1)}$ and residual $\varepsilon^{(k+1)}$ from \Cref{eq:true-step-definitions,eq:residual-definition} satisfy $\|\varepsilon^{(k+1)}\|_M \leq \sigma \|s^{(k+1)}\|_M$\;
        $v^{(k+1)} \gets \prox_{\gamma_x f}(\xi^{(k+1)} - \gamma_x A^\top \nu^{(k+1)})$\;
        $z^{(k+1)} \gets z^{(k)} + \theta s^{(k+1)}$\;
    }
\end{algorithm}
The test can always be met: as the inner method converges, $\varepsilon \to 0$ while $s \to s^\star(z^{(k)})$, which is nonzero unless $z^{(k)}$ is already a fixed point.
\Cref{sec:linear-system} bounds how many inner iterations the test requires.

\subsection{Motivation}
The method is a variant of the relative-error Douglas--Rachford method of \citet{EcksteinRelativeError2018}.
To motivate it, we write down the KKT conditions of \Cref{eq:linearly-constrained-problem}.
A point $x\in\R^n$ is optimal for \Cref{eq:linearly-constrained-problem} if and only if there exists $\lambda\in\R^m$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kkt-conditions}
    -A^\top \lambda \in \partial f (x), \qquad Ax = b.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $(x, \lambda)$ solves the monotone inclusion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monotone-inclusion-kkt}
    0 \in \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
        \partial f (x) \\ 0
    \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathcal{F}(x,\lambda)} + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
        A^\top \lambda \\ b - Ax
    \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathcal{G}(x,\lambda)}.
\end{equation}
The operator $\mathcal{F}$ is maximal monotone because $f$ is closed, proper, and convex, and $\mathcal{G}$ is maximal monotone because it is an affine map whose linear part is skew-symmetric \citep{BauschkeConvexAnalysisResolventsOfMonotoneOperators}.

\subsubsection{Choice of Metric}
Inspired by \citet{alves2025generalframeworkinexactsplitting}, we work in the metric induced by
\begin{equation*}
    M = \begin{bmatrix}
    \gamma_x^{-1} I & 0 \\ 0 & \gamma_\lambda^{-1} I
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The resolvent of $\mathcal{F}$ in this metric is a proximal step on the primal variable alone,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f-resolvent}
    (I+M^{-1}\mathcal{F})^{-1}(x,\lambda) = (\prox_{\gamma_x f}(x), \lambda).
\end{equation}
For $\mathcal{G}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-forward-step}
    (I + M^{-1}\mathcal{G})(x,\lambda) = \begin{bmatrix} x + \gamma_x A^\top\lambda \\ \lambda + \gamma_\lambda(b-Ax)
    \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
        I & \gamma_x A^\top \\ -\gamma_\lambda A & I
    \end{bmatrix}}_{H}\begin{bmatrix}
        x \\ \lambda
    \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}
        0 \\ \gamma_\lambda b
    \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
so the resolvent of $\mathcal{G}$ is the solution of a linear system,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-resolvent}
    (I + M^{-1}\mathcal{G})^{-1}(x,\lambda) = H^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}
        x \\ \lambda - \gamma_\lambda b
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The matrix $H$ is invertible for all $\gamma_x, \gamma_\lambda > 0$, since its Schur complement $I + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda AA^\top$ is positive definite.
Keeping $\gamma_x$ and $\gamma_\lambda$ separate decouples the proximal step size in \Cref{eq:f-resolvent} from the scaling of the linear system in \Cref{eq:g-resolvent}, which gives control over the spectrum of that system.
\Cref{sec:linear-system-analysis} makes this intuition precise, and \Cref{lem:singular-values-H} explicitly describes the singular values of $H$ in terms of the singular values of $A$.

\subsubsection{Exact and inexact steps}
Fix $z = (x, \lambda) \in \R^n \times \R^m$ and let $(\xi, \nu) \in \R^n \times \R^m$ be any approximate solution of the linear system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-system-step}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I & \gamma_x A^\top \\ -\gamma_\lambda A & I
    \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
        \xi \\ \nu
    \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
        x \\ \lambda - \gamma_\lambda b
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:true-step-definitions}
    v = \prox_{\gamma_x f}(\xi - \gamma_x A^\top \nu), \qquad
    \hat{z} = \begin{bmatrix} v + \gamma_x A^\top \nu \\ \nu \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    s = \begin{bmatrix} v - \xi \\ \gamma_\lambda (A\xi - b) \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-definition}
    \varepsilon = \hat{z} - z - s = \begin{bmatrix} \xi + \gamma_x A^\top \nu - x \\ \nu - \gamma_\lambda A\xi - \lambda + \gamma_\lambda b \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
which is the residual of \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} at $(\xi, \nu)$.
When the system is solved exactly, $\varepsilon = 0$, the point $\xi - \gamma_x A^\top\nu = 2\xi - x$ is the reflection of $x$ through the resolvent of $\mathcal{G}$, $\hat z$ is the Douglas--Rachford image of $z$, and $s = \hat z - z$ is the Douglas--Rachford step.
When the system is solved inexactly, the quantities in \Cref{eq:true-step-definitions} are \emph{defined} by the formulas, which use $\xi - \gamma_x A^\top \nu$ and $\gamma_\lambda(A\xi - b)$ in place of $2\xi - x$ and $\lambda - 2\nu$; the two agree only for the exact solution, and it is the formulas that the analysis below refers to.

\begin{definition}\label{def:admissible-pairs}
We call $(\hat z, s)$ an \emph{admissible pair} if it arises from some $(\xi, \nu)$ through \Cref{eq:true-step-definitions}, and we write $\mathcal{A}$ for the set of admissible pairs.
The set $\mathcal{A}$ does not depend on $z$; the point $z$ enters only through the residual $\varepsilon$.
Let
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{Z}^\star = \{ (x^\star + \gamma_x A^\top\lambda^\star, \lambda^\star) \mid (x^\star,\lambda^\star) \text{ satisfies } \Cref{eq:kkt-conditions} \}
\end{equation*}
denote the set of fixed points, which is nonempty by \Cref{ass:standing}.
Finally, let $s^\star(z)$ denote the step \Cref{eq:true-step-definitions} obtained from the exact solution of \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} at $z$; this is the step of the exact Douglas--Rachford method, and $s^\star(z) = 0$ if and only if $z \in \mathcal{Z}^\star$.    
\end{definition}

\subsection{Convergence}
The analysis rests on one lemma, which says that admissible pairs inherit the monotonicity of the Douglas--Rachford operator whether or not the linear system was solved exactly.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:monotonicity}
    The following statements hold.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item For any two admissible pairs $(\hat z_1, s_1)$ and $(\hat z_2, s_2)$,
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:acc-two-point}
            \langle \hat z_1 - \hat z_2, s_1 - s_2\rangle_M \leq 0.
        \end{equation}
        \item For every $z^\star \in \mathcal{Z}^\star$, the pair $(z^\star, 0)$ is admissible.
        Moreover, if $(\hat z, s)$ is the admissible pair generated by $(\xi, \nu)$ and $z^\star = (x^\star + \gamma_x A^\top\lambda^\star, \lambda^\star)$, then
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:montonicity-of-update}
            \langle \hat z - z^\star, s\rangle_M = -\langle v - x^\star, u - u^\star\rangle
        \end{equation}
        for the subgradients $u = \gamma_x^{-1}(\xi - v) - A^\top\nu \in \partial f(v)$ and $u^\star = -A^\top\lambda^\star \in \partial f(x^\star)$.
        \item Let $(\hat z, s)$ be admissible with residual $\varepsilon$ at $z$.
        Then $\|s - s^\star(z)\|_M \leq \|\varepsilon\|_M$.
        In particular, if $\|\varepsilon\|_M \leq \sigma\|s\|_M$ with $\sigma < 1$, then
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:acc-exact-vs-inexact}
            (1-\sigma)\|s\|_M \leq \|s^\star(z)\|_M \leq (1+\sigma)\|s\|_M.
        \end{equation}
        \item For any $z_1, z_2$, the exact steps satisfy $\|s^\star(z_1) - s^\star(z_2)\|_M \leq \|z_1 - z_2\|_M$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For an admissible pair generated by $(\xi_i, \nu_i)$, the optimality condition of the proximal step reads
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:acc-prox-optimality}
        u_i := \gamma_x^{-1}(\xi_i - v_i) - A^\top\nu_i \in \partial f(v_i).
    \end{equation}
    Expanding the $M$-inner product and cancelling the terms in $A\xi_i$,
    \begin{align*}
        \langle \hat z_1 - \hat z_2, s_1 - s_2\rangle_M
        &= \gamma_x^{-1}\langle v_1 - v_2 + \gamma_x A^\top(\nu_1 - \nu_2),\, (v_1 - \xi_1) - (v_2 - \xi_2)\rangle
           + \langle \nu_1 - \nu_2,\, A(\xi_1 - \xi_2)\rangle \\
        &= \gamma_x^{-1}\langle v_1 - v_2,\, (v_1 - \xi_1) - (v_2 - \xi_2)\rangle
           + \langle \nu_1 - \nu_2,\, A(v_1 - v_2)\rangle \\
        &= \left\langle v_1 - v_2,\, \gamma_x^{-1}\big((v_1 - \xi_1) - (v_2 - \xi_2)\big) + A^\top(\nu_1 - \nu_2)\right\rangle \\
        &= -\langle v_1 - v_2,\, u_1 - u_2\rangle \leq 0,
    \end{align*}
    where the last inequality is the monotonicity of $\partial f$.
    This proves the first statement.

    For the second statement take $\xi = x^\star$ and $\nu = \lambda^\star$.
    Since $-A^\top\lambda^\star \in \partial f(x^\star)$, the proximal step returns $v = \prox_{\gamma_x f}(x^\star - \gamma_x A^\top\lambda^\star) = x^\star$.
    Hence $\hat z = (x^\star + \gamma_x A^\top\lambda^\star, \lambda^\star) = z^\star$ and $s = (0, \gamma_\lambda(Ax^\star - b)) = 0$, and the pair is generated by $(x^\star, \lambda^\star)$ with subgradient $u^\star = \gamma_x^{-1}(x^\star - x^\star) - A^\top\lambda^\star = -A^\top\lambda^\star$.
    The identity \Cref{eq:montonicity-of-update} is the computation above with $(\hat z_1, s_1) = (\hat z, s)$ and $(\hat z_2, s_2) = (z^\star, 0)$.

    For the third statement, $\hat z = z + s + \varepsilon$ and $\hat z^\star = z + s^\star(z)$ give $\varepsilon = (\hat z - \hat z^\star) - (s - s^\star(z))$.
    Expanding the square and using \Cref{eq:acc-two-point},
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:true-update-drift-inequality}
        \|\varepsilon\|_M^2 = \|\hat z - \hat z^\star\|_M^2 - 2\langle \hat z - \hat z^\star, s - s^\star(z)\rangle_M + \|s - s^\star(z)\|_M^2 \geq \|s - s^\star(z)\|_M^2.
    \end{equation}
    The two-sided bound \Cref{eq:acc-exact-vs-inexact} follows from the triangle inequality.

    For the fourth statement, the exact pairs at $z_1$ and $z_2$ are $(z_i + s^\star(z_i), s^\star(z_i))$, and \Cref{eq:acc-two-point} gives
    \begin{equation*}
        \|s^\star(z_1) - s^\star(z_2)\|_M^2 \leq -\langle z_1 - z_2, s^\star(z_1) - s^\star(z_2)\rangle_M \leq \|z_1 - z_2\|_M \|s^\star(z_1) - s^\star(z_2)\|_M,
    \end{equation*}
    which is the claim after dividing by $\|s^\star(z_1) - s^\star(z_2)\|_M$ when that is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One-step inequality]\label{thm:lyapunov-inequality}
    Let $z^\star \in \mathcal{Z}^\star$, let $(\hat z, s)$ be an admissible pair with residual $\varepsilon$ at $z$, and let $z^+ = z + \theta s$ with $\theta > 0$.
    Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:inexactness-one-step-inequality}
        \|z^{+} - z^\star\|_M^2 \leq \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 - \theta(2-\theta)\|s\|^2_M - 2\theta \langle\varepsilon, s\rangle_M,
    \end{equation}
    and more precisely, with $u$, $u^\star$ as in \Cref{lem:monotonicity},
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:one-step-identity}
        \|z^{+} - z^\star\|_M^2 = \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 - \theta(2-\theta)\|s\|^2_M - 2\theta \langle\varepsilon, s\rangle_M - 2\theta\langle v - x^\star, u - u^\star\rangle.
    \end{equation}
    % For $\theta = 1$, with $q = \hat z - z = s + \varepsilon$, inequality \Cref{eq:inexactness-one-step-inequality} reads
    % \begin{equation}\label{eq:one-step-inequality-no-relaxation}
        % \|z^{+} - z^\star\|_M^2 \leq \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 - \| q\|_M^2 + \|\varepsilon\|_M^2.
    % \end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Since $z = \hat z - s - \varepsilon$,
    \begin{align}
        \|z + \theta s - z^\star\|_M^2
        &= \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 + 2\theta\langle z - z^\star, s\rangle_M + \theta^2\|s\|_M^2 \\
        &= \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 + 2\theta\langle \hat z - z^\star, s\rangle_M - 2\theta\|s\|_M^2 - 2\theta\langle \varepsilon, s\rangle_M + \theta^2\|s\|_M^2,
    \end{align}
    and \Cref{eq:montonicity-of-update} turns the second term into $-2\theta\langle v - x^\star, u - u^\star\rangle$, which proves \Cref{eq:one-step-identity}; that term is nonpositive by the monotonicity of $\partial f$, which proves \Cref{eq:inexactness-one-step-inequality}.
    For $\theta = 1$, $-\|s\|_M^2 - 2\langle\varepsilon, s\rangle_M = \|\varepsilon\|_M^2 - \|s + \varepsilon\|_M^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Decrease under the relative-error test]\label{cor:over-under-relaxation}
    In the setting of \Cref{thm:lyapunov-inequality}, if $\|\varepsilon\|_M \leq \sigma\|s\|_M$, then
    \begin{align}\label{eq:lyapunov-decrease-estimate}
        \|z^{+} - z^\star\|_M^2 &\leq \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 - \theta(2-\theta-2\sigma)\|s\|^2_M - 2\theta\langle v - x^\star, u - u^\star\rangle \\
        &\leq \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 - \theta(2-\theta-2\sigma)\|s\|^2_M.
    \end{align}
    The coefficient $\theta(2 - \theta - 2\sigma)$ is positive if and only if $\theta \in (0, 2)$ and $\sigma < (2 - \theta)/2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $-2\theta\langle\varepsilon, s\rangle_M \leq 2\theta\sigma\|s\|_M^2$.
    Substituting this into \Cref{eq:one-step-identity} yields the desired result.
\end{proof}
The corollary explains the role of the tolerance.
Over-relaxation ($\theta > 1$) enlarges the step, and the exact method may use any $\theta \in (0, 2)$; inexactness consumes part of the same margin, so the admissible tolerance shrinks linearly in $\theta$, to zero at $\theta = 2$.
Conversely, at $\theta = 1$ the linear system may be solved to any relative accuracy $\sigma < 1/2$ without losing the decrease of $\|z^{(k)} - z^\star\|_M$.

\begin{theorem}[Summability of the fixed-point residual]\label{thm:relative-error-criterion}
    Let $\theta \in (0, 2)$ and $\sigma \in [0, (2-\theta)/2)$, and let $(z^{(k)})$ be generated by \Cref{alg:main}.
    Then for every $z^\star \in \mathcal{Z}^\star$ the sequence $\|z^{(k)} - z^\star\|_M$ is nonincreasing,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:convergence-of-fixed-point-residual}
        \sum_{t=0}^{k}\|s^{(t+1)}\|^2_M \leq \frac{\|z^{(0)} - z^\star\|_M^2 - \|z^{(k+1)} - z^\star\|_M^2}{\theta(2-\theta-2\sigma)}
        \quad\text{for every } k,
    \end{equation}
    and consequently $\|s^{(k)}\|_M \to 0$, $\|\varepsilon^{(k)}\|_M \to 0$, and
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:small-o-rate}
        \min_{1 \leq t \leq k} \|s^{(t)}\|_M^2 = o(1/k).
    \end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Summing \Cref{eq:lyapunov-decrease-estimate} over the iterations $0, \ldots, k$ gives \Cref{eq:convergence-of-fixed-point-residual}, and each term of the sum is nonnegative, so the series $\sum_t \|s^{(t)}\|_M^2$ converges; hence $\|s^{(k)}\|_M \to 0$ and $\|\varepsilon^{(k)}\|_M \leq \sigma\|s^{(k)}\|_M \to 0$.
    Moreover, \Cref{cor:over-under-relaxation} shows that $\|z^{(k)} - z^\star\|_M$ is nonincreasing.
    For \Cref{eq:small-o-rate}, let $a_t = \|s^{(t)}\|_M^2$.
    Then
    \begin{equation*}
        \frac{k}{2}\min_{1 \leq t \leq k} a_t \leq \sum_{t = \lceil k/2 \rceil}^{k} a_t \to 0 \quad\text{as } k \to \infty,
    \end{equation*}
    because the tail of a convergent series tends to zero.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
    The quantity $\|s^{(k+1)}\|_M$ is the fixed-point residual of the inexact iteration, and by \Cref{eq:acc-exact-vs-inexact} it is within a factor $1 \pm \sigma$ of the exact residual $\|s^\star(z^{(k)})\|_M$.
    \Cref{thm:relative-error-criterion} therefore controls the exact residual as well.
    Convergence of $z^{(k)}$ itself to a point of $\mathcal{Z}^\star$ follows from the standard Fej\'er-monotonicity argument once the residual vanishes; we omit it and turn to rates.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Linear convergence]\label{thm:linear-convergence}
    Let $\theta \in (0,2)$, $\sigma \in [0, (2-\theta)/2)$, let $A$ have full row rank, and let $f$ be $\mu$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth.
    Then $\mathcal{Z}^\star = \{z^\star\}$ is a singleton and the iterates of \Cref{alg:main} satisfy
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-rate}
        \|z^{(k+1)} - z^\star\|_M^2 \leq \left(1 - C^{-1}\right)\|z^{(k)} - z^\star\|_M^2,
    \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{subequations}\label{eq:linear-rate-constants}
        \begin{align}
        D &= 1 + \sigma + \sqrt{1 + (\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda \sigma_{\min}(A)^2)^{-1}} \\
        K &= \frac{2(\mu + L)}{\mu L \gamma_x} + 2(\mu + L)\left(\gamma_x + \frac{1}{\gamma_\lambda \sigma_{\min}(A)^2}\right) \\
        C &= \frac{K}{\theta} + \frac{2D^2}{\theta(2-\theta-2\sigma)}.
        \end{align}
    \end{subequations}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Uniqueness of the KKT point follows from strong convexity ($x^\star$ is unique) and full row rank ($\lambda^\star$ is determined by $A^\top\lambda^\star = -\nabla f(x^\star)$).
    We fix an outer iteration and drop the superscripts: $z = z^{(k)}$, $z^+ = z^{(k+1)}$, and $(\xi, \nu, v, \hat z, s, \varepsilon, q)$ are the quantities of \Cref{eq:true-step-definitions,eq:residual-definition} at iteration $k$.
    Since $f$ is differentiable, we may define $u = \nabla f(v)$ and $u^\star = \nabla f(x^\star)$, and write $g = \nabla f(v) - \nabla f(x^\star)$.

    Firstly, by \Cref{eq:lyapunov-decrease-estimate} and \cite[Theorem 2.1.12]{nesterov2004introductory}
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:l-smooth-mu-strongly-convex-inequality}
        \langle v - x^\star, \nabla f(v) - \nabla f(x^\star)\rangle \geq \frac{\mu L}{\mu + L} \|v - x^\star\|^2 + \frac{1}{\mu+L}\|\nabla f(v) - \nabla f(x^\star)\|^2,
    \end{equation}
    we have
    \begin{align}\label{eq:decrease-delta}
        \|z^+ - z^\star\|_M^2 &\leq \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 - \underbrace{\theta(2-\theta-2\sigma)\|s\|_M^2 + \frac{2\theta\mu L}{\mu + L}\|v - x^\star\|^2 + \frac{2\theta}{\mu + L}\|g\|^2}_{=:\delta}.
    \end{align}

    Write $s = (s_x, s_\lambda)$.
    From $\hat{z} - z^\star = (v - x^\star + \gamma_x A^\top(\nu - \lambda^\star),\ \nu - \lambda^\star)$, the optimality condition \Cref{eq:acc-prox-optimality}, and the identity $A^\top\lambda^\star = -\nabla f(x^\star)$,
    \begin{equation*}
        A^\top(\nu - \lambda^\star) = \gamma_x^{-1}(\xi - v) - \nabla f(v) + \nabla f(x^\star) = -\gamma_x^{-1} s_x - g.
    \end{equation*}
    Since $A$ has full row rank, 
    \[\|\nu - \lambda^\star\| \leq \sigma_{\min}(A)^{-1}\|A^\top(\nu - \lambda^\star)\| \leq \sigma_{\min}(A)^{-1}(\gamma_x^{-1}\|s_x\| + \|g\|).\]
    Split $\hat z - z^\star$ into the three vectors $(v - x^\star, 0)$, $(-s_x, w_1)$, and $(-\gamma_x g, w_2)$, where $w_1 + w_2 = \nu - \lambda^\star$ is the corresponding split of the multiplier component with $\|w_1\| \leq \sigma_{\min}(A)^{-1}\gamma_x^{-1}\|s_x\|$ and $\|w_2\| \leq \sigma_{\min}(A)^{-1}\|g\|$.
    The triangle inequality in $\|\cdot\|_M$ and the bound $\gamma_x^{-1/2}\|s_x\| \leq \|s\|_M$ give
    \begin{equation*}
        \|\hat z - z^\star\|_M \leq \gamma_x^{-1/2}\|v - x^\star\| + \sqrt{\gamma_x + \frac{1}{\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\min}(A)^2}}\,\|g\| + \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\min}(A)^2}}\,\|s\|_M.
    \end{equation*}
    Since $z - z^\star = \hat z - z^\star - q$ and $\|q\|_M \leq \|s\|_M + \|\varepsilon\|_M \leq (1 + \sigma)\|s\|_M$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:lyapunov-bound}
        \|z - z^\star\|_M \leq \gamma_x^{-1/2}\|v - x^\star\| + \sqrt{\gamma_x + \frac{1}{\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\min}(A)^2}}\,\|g\| + D\|s\|_M.
    \end{equation}
    Squaring with $(a + b + c)^2 \leq 4a^2 + 4b^2 + 2c^2$,
    \begin{equation*}
        \|z - z^\star\|_M^2 \leq \frac{4}{\gamma_x}\|v - x^\star\|^2 + 4\left(\gamma_x + \frac{1}{\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\min}(A)^2}\right)\|g\|^2 + 2D^2\|s\|_M^2 \leq C\,\delta,
    \end{equation*}
    where the last step compares coefficients with \Cref{eq:decrease-delta}: the three inequalities
    \begin{align*}
        \frac{4}{\gamma_x} &\leq C\,\frac{2\theta\mu L}{\mu + L} \\
        4\left(\gamma_x + \frac{1}{\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\min}(A)^2}\right) &\leq C\,\frac{2\theta}{\mu + L} \\
        2D^2 &\leq C\,\theta(2 - \theta - 2\sigma)
    \end{align*}
    hold because $C \geq K/\theta$ for the first two and $C \geq 2D^2/(\theta(2-\theta-2\sigma))$ for the third.
    Hence $\delta \geq C^{-1}\|z - z^\star\|_M^2$.
    Applying this lower bound to \Cref{eq:decrease-delta} yields the theorem.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
    If $A$ does not have full row rank, the same argument holds, but with $\dist_M(z^{(k)}, \mathcal{Z}^\star)$ instead of $\|z^{(k)} - z^\star\|_M$, $\sigma_{\min}^+(A)$ in place of $\sigma_{\min}(A)$, and the multiplier component measured in the range of $A$, since $\lambda^\star$ is not unique in this case.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}[Choosing the step size]\label{rem:step-size}
    With $\gamma_x = \gamma_\lambda = \gamma$, the constant $K$ in \Cref{eq:linear-rate-constants} becomes $2(\mu + L)\left(\gamma + (1/(\mu L) + 1/\sigma_{\min}(A)^2)/\gamma\right)$, which is minimized at
    \begin{equation*}
        \gamma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\mu L} + \frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}(A)^2}},
    \end{equation*}
    while $D$ decreases in $\gamma$.
    The value above is a simple heuristic for the optimal value.
    The minimizer of $C$ therefore lies at or above this value, and a search algorithm like bisection over $\gamma$ may be used to find it.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
    The bound says nothing about the cost of the inner solve, which grows with $\gamma$.
    This is addressed theoretically in \Cref{sec:linear-system} and the experimentally in \Cref{sec:experiments-qp}.
\end{remark}

\section{The Linear System}\label{sec:linear-system}
The method would be a routine variant of relative-error Douglas--Rachford were it not for the following: because the linear system \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} is written directly in terms of $A$, its spectrum is known explicitly as a function of $\gamma_x$, $\gamma_\lambda$, and the singular values of $A$.
This makes it possible to bound the number of inner iterations that the relative-error test demands for a given inner solver, and hence the total inner work over a fixed number of outer iterations, in the same terms as the outer analysis.

\subsection{Inner Work}
Throughout this subsection, $\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}$ and $s^{(k)}_{(l)}$ denote the residual and the step of \Cref{eq:true-step-definitions,eq:residual-definition} at the $l$th inner iterate of the system at $z^{(k)}$, and
\begin{equation*}
    \bar{R}_k = \|s^\star(z^{(k)})\|_M, \qquad \mathcal{E}_{k,0} = \|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(0)}\|_M,
\end{equation*}
are the norm of the exact step and the residual of the warm start at outer iteration $k$.
We assume $\bar R_k > 0$, that is, the method has not reached a fixed point; otherwise the warm start passes the test at once.

\begin{theorem}[Stopping time of the inner solve]\label{thm:stopping-time}
    Suppose the inner solver contracts the residual geometrically in expectation,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:inner-contraction}
        \mathbb{E}\left[\|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|^2_M\right] \leq a^l\,\mathcal{E}_{k,0}^2 \quad\text{for all } l \geq 0,
    \end{equation}
    for some $a \in (0, 1)$, and let $\sigma > 0$.
    Define the stopping time
    \begin{equation*}
        N_k = \inf\{l \geq 0 : \|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M \leq \sigma\|s^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M\},
    \end{equation*}
    the first inner iteration at which the iterate is accepted.
    Then,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:stopping-time-probability-bound}
        \Prob(N_k > l) \leq \beta_k a^l,
    \end{equation}
    where $ \beta_k = \left(\frac{(1 + \sigma)\mathcal{E}_{k,0}}{\sigma \bar{R}_k}\right)^2$.
    Thus,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:stopping-time-expectation-bound}
        \mathbb{E}[N_k] \leq \left\lceil \frac{\logp\beta_k}{\log (1/a)} \right\rceil + \frac{1}{1-a}.
    \end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    By \Cref{lem:monotonicity}, $\|s^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M \geq \bar R_k - \|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M$ for every inner iterate.
    On the event $\{N_k > l\}$ the test fails at $l$, so
    \begin{equation*}
        \|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M > \sigma\|s^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M \geq \sigma\bar R_k - \sigma\|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M,
    \end{equation*}
    so $\|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|_M > \frac{\sigma}{1 + \sigma}\bar R_k$.
    Markov's inequality applied to $\|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|^2_M$ and \Cref{eq:inner-contraction} give
    \begin{equation*}
        \Prob(N_k > l) \leq \Prob\left(\|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|^2_M > \left(\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^2 \bar R_k^2\right)
        \leq \frac{(1+\sigma)^2\,\mathbb{E}[\|\varepsilon^{(k)}_{(l)}\|^2_M]}{\sigma^2 \bar R_k^2}
        \leq \beta_k a^l,
    \end{equation*}
    which is \Cref{eq:stopping-time-probability-bound}.
    For the expectation, let $L_k = \lceil \logp\beta_k / \log(1/a) \rceil$, so that $\beta_k a^{l} \leq 1$ for $l \geq L_k$.
    Since $N_k$ is a nonnegative integer,
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathbb{E}[N_k] = \sum_{l=0}^\infty \Prob(N_k>l) \leq \sum_{l=0}^\infty \min\{1,\beta_k a^l\}
        \leq L_k + \sum_{l = L_k}^{\infty} \beta_k a^l = L_k + \frac{\beta_k a^{L_k}}{1-a} \leq L_k + \frac{1}{1-a}.
    \end{equation*}
\end{proof}
The bound \Cref{eq:stopping-time-expectation-bound} has two parts: the number of inner iterations needed to bring the warm-start residual down to the scale $\sigma \bar R_k$ of the test, which grows only logarithmically in $1/\sigma$ and in the ratio $\mathcal{E}_{k,0}/\bar R_k$, and a constant $1/(1-a)$ that accounts for the randomness of the solver.
For a deterministic solver such as conjugate gradients, \Cref{eq:inner-contraction} holds without the expectation and the same bound applies.

In practice, one would wish to warm start the inner solver, as the solutions to the linear system may lie on a trajectory with little drift.
It is clear that the ratio $\mathcal{E}_{k,0}/\bar R_k$ is controlled by the choice of warm start, but we can surprisingly show that the solution drift is bounded by constants relating to the problem data.
\begin{lemma}[Warm start and drift]\label{lem:warm-start-drift}
    Let the system at $z^{(k)}$, $k \geq 1$, be warm started at $(\xi^{(k)}, \nu^{(k)})$, the iterate accepted at the previous outer iteration.
    Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:warm-start-residual}
        \mathcal{E}_{k,0} \leq \|\varepsilon^{(k)}\|_M + \theta\|s^{(k)}\|_M \leq (\sigma + \theta)\|s^{(k)}\|_M \leq \frac{\sigma + \theta}{1 - \sigma}\,\bar R_{k-1},
    \end{equation}
    and the exact step norms satisfy
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-step-growth}
        \bar R_{k} \leq \bar R_{k-1} + \theta\|s^{(k)}\|_M \leq \left(1 + \frac{\theta}{1-\sigma}\right)\bar R_{k-1}.
    \end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The right-hand side of \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} at $z$ is $z - (0, \gamma_\lambda b)$, so it changes by $z^{(k)} - z^{(k-1)} = \theta s^{(k)}$ between consecutive outer iterations, while the warm start is the same point $(\xi^{(k)}, \nu^{(k)})$.
    Its residual at the new system is therefore $\varepsilon^{(k)} - \theta s^{(k)}$, and the first inequality of \Cref{eq:warm-start-residual} is the triangle inequality; the second is the relative-error test and the third is \Cref{eq:acc-exact-vs-inexact}, which gives $\|s^{(k)}\|_M \leq \bar R_{k-1}/(1 - \sigma)$.
    For \Cref{eq:exact-step-growth}, \Cref{lem:monotonicity}, item 4, gives $\|s^\star(z^{(k)}) - s^\star(z^{(k-1)})\|_M \leq \|z^{(k)} - z^{(k-1)}\|_M = \theta\|s^{(k)}\|_M$, and the triangle inequality and \Cref{eq:acc-exact-vs-inexact} finish the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Total inner work]\label{thm:total-inner-work}
    Under the assumptions of \Cref{thm:stopping-time,lem:warm-start-drift}, with $\rho = (1+\sigma)(\sigma+\theta)/(\sigma(1-\sigma))$, the total inner work over the first $n$ outer iterations satisfies
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:total-inner-work-estimate}
        \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} N_k\right] \leq n\left(1 + \frac{1}{1-a}\right) + \frac{1}{\log(1/a)}\left(\logp\beta_0 + 2(n-1)\log\!\left(\rho\left(1 + \frac{\theta}{1-\sigma}\right)\right) + 2\log\frac{\bar{R}_{0}}{\bar{R}_{n-1}}\right).
    \end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Summing \Cref{eq:stopping-time-expectation-bound} and using $\lceil t \rceil \leq t + 1$,
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} N_k\right] \leq n\left(1 + \frac{1}{1-a}\right) + \frac{1}{\log(1/a)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\logp\beta_k.
    \end{equation*}
    For $k \geq 1$, \Cref{eq:warm-start-residual} gives $\beta_k \leq \rho^2 (\bar R_{k-1}/\bar R_k)^2$, and since $\rho \geq 1$ and $\logp(xy) \leq \logp x + \logp y$,
    \begin{equation*}
        \logp\beta_k \leq 2\log\rho + 2\logp\frac{\bar R_{k-1}}{\bar R_k}.
    \end{equation*}
    Using $\logp t = \log t + \logp(1/t)$ for $t > 0$ and then \Cref{eq:exact-step-growth},
    \begin{equation*}
        \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\logp\frac{\bar R_{k-1}}{\bar R_k}
        = \log\frac{\bar R_0}{\bar R_{n-1}} + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\logp\frac{\bar R_{k}}{\bar R_{k-1}}
        \leq \log\frac{\bar R_0}{\bar R_{n-1}} + (n-1)\log\left(1 + \frac{\theta}{1-\sigma}\right).
    \end{equation*}
    Combining the three displays yields \Cref{eq:total-inner-work-estimate}.
\end{proof}
The bound has the shape that the experiments in \Cref{sec:numerical-results} exhibit: a constant per outer iteration that depends on the tolerance through $\log\rho = O(\log(1/\sigma))$ and on the inner solver through $a$, plus a single logarithmic term $\log(\bar R_0/\bar R_{n-1})$ that accounts for the total progress of the outer iteration.
The only quantity that is not explicit is $\beta_0$, the residual of whatever warm start the first system is given.

\begin{remark}[Per-iteration bound and randomized Kaczmarz]\label{rem:per-iteration}
    If the exact step norms do not drop too quickly between consecutive iterations, $\sup_k \bar R_{k-1}/\bar R_{k} \leq \Delta < \infty$, then \Cref{eq:stopping-time-expectation-bound,eq:warm-start-residual} give the iteration-independent bound
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathbb{E}[N_k] \leq 1 + \frac{1}{1-a} + \frac{2\log(\rho\Delta)}{\log(1/a)} \quad\text{for } k \geq 1.
    \end{equation*}
    Randomized Kaczmarz \citep{strohmer2007randomizedkaczmarzalgorithmexponential} applied to the positive definite system $S\nu = r$ with $S = I + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda AA^\top$ of \Cref{sec:linear-system-analysis} contracts the error as $\mathbb{E}\|e_l\|^2 \leq (1 - \lambda_{\min}(S)^2/\|S\|_F^2)^l\|e_0\|^2$.
    Since $\|\varepsilon\| \leq \|S\|\|e\|$ and $\|e_0\| \leq \|S^{-1}\|\|\varepsilon_0\|$, assumption \Cref{eq:inner-contraction} holds for the residual with $a = 1 - \lambda_{\min}(S)^2/\|S\|_F^2$ and $\mathcal{E}_{k,0}$ replaced by $\kappa(S)\mathcal{E}_{k,0}$, which adds $2\log\kappa(S)$ to the logarithmic term.
    With $\lambda_{\min}(S) \geq 1$ and $\|S\|_F^2 = \sum_{i}(1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_i(A)^2)^2 \leq m(1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\max}(A)^2)^2$, this gives $1/(1-a) \leq m(1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\max}(A)^2)^2$, so the expected number of row projections per outer iteration is $O\big(m(1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\max}(A)^2)^2\log(\kappa(S)\rho\Delta)\big)$: linear in $m$ at a fixed spectrum, and growing with the step sizes.
    Both trends appear in \Cref{sec:experiments-lasso}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Spectrum of the Linear System}\label{sec:linear-system-analysis}
Recall the system \Cref{eq:linear-system-step},
\begin{equation*}
    \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} I & \gamma_x A^\top \\ -\gamma_\lambda A & I \end{bmatrix}}_H\begin{bmatrix} \xi \\ \nu \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ \lambda - \gamma_\lambda b \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
It can be solved as it stands, by a method for nonsymmetric systems such as GMRES, or through its Schur complement, which is symmetric positive definite.

\subsubsection{The Asymmetric System}
The asymmetric system inherits the sparsity of $A$ is, and its singular values can be explicitly described in terms of the stepsizes $\gamma_x,\gamma_\lambda$ and the singular values of $A$.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:singular-values-H}
    Recall that we defined $r := \Rank(A)$.
    The $(n+m)\times(n+m)$ matrix $H$ has, for each $i = 1, \ldots, r$, the two singular values
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:singular-values-H}
        \frac{\sqrt{4 + (\gamma_x+\gamma_\lambda)^2\sigma_i(A)^2} \pm |\gamma_x-\gamma_\lambda|\,\sigma_i(A)}{2},
    \end{equation}
    and $n + m - 2r$ singular values equal to $1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Let $A = U\Sigma V^\top$ be a full singular value decomposition of $A$, with $U \in \R^{m \times m}$ and $V \in \R^{n\times n}$ orthogonal, and write $\sigma_i = \sigma_i(A)$.
    Then
    \begin{equation*}
        H = \begin{bmatrix}
                V & 0 \\ 0 & U
        \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
                I & \gamma_x \Sigma^\top \\ -\gamma_\lambda \Sigma & I
        \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
                V^\top & 0 \\ 0 & U^\top
        \end{bmatrix},
    \end{equation*}
    so it suffices to find the singular values of the middle matrix.
    After a symmetric permutation that pairs the $i$th column of $V$ with the $i$th column of $U$, the middle matrix is block diagonal with $r$ blocks
    \begin{equation*}
        B_i = \begin{bmatrix}
            1 & \gamma_x \sigma_i \\
            -\gamma_\lambda \sigma_i & 1
        \end{bmatrix}, \quad i = 1, \ldots, r,
    \end{equation*}
    followed by an identity block of size $n + m - 2r$.
    Each block has $\|B_i\|_F^2 = 2 + \sigma_i^2(\gamma_\lambda^2 + \gamma_x^2)$ and $|\det B_i| = 1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda \sigma_i^2$.
    If $s_1 \geq s_2$ are the singular values of $B_i$, then $s_1^2 + s_2^2 = \|B_i\|_F^2$ and $s_1 s_2 = |\det B_i|$, so
    \begin{equation*}
        (s_1+s_2)^2 = 4 + (\gamma_x+\gamma_\lambda)^2\sigma_i^2,
        \qquad
        (s_1-s_2)^2 = (\gamma_x-\gamma_\lambda)^2\sigma_i^2,
    \end{equation*}
    and solving for $s_1$ and $s_2$ gives \Cref{eq:singular-values-H}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}\label{rem:M-scaled-singular-values}
    The same argument shows that $M^{1/2} H M^{-1/2} = \begin{bmatrix} I & \sqrt{\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda}A^\top \\ -\sqrt{\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda}A & I\end{bmatrix}$ has the singular value $\sqrt{1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_i(A)^2}$ with multiplicity $2$ for each $i = 1, \ldots, r$, and the singular value $1$ with multiplicity $n+m-2r$.
    In the metric $M$ the coupled system therefore has the same conditioning as the Schur complement below.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
    In the Euclidean norm, however, $H$ can be poorly conditioned even when $A$ is not.
    Minimizing the smaller value in \Cref{eq:singular-values-H} over $\sigma_i(A)$ shows that $\sigma_{\min}(H)$ is no smaller than $\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda}}{\gamma_x+\gamma_\lambda}$, with equality when $\sigma_i(A) = \frac{|\gamma_x - \gamma_\lambda|}{(\gamma_x + \gamma_\lambda)\sqrt{\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda}}$ for some $i$.
    This value is $1$ when $\gamma_x = \gamma_\lambda$ and tends to zero as the two step sizes separate.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The Positive Definite System}
Block elimination gives
\begin{equation*}
    H = \begin{bmatrix}
        I & 0 \\
        -\gamma_\lambda A & I
    \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
        I & 0 \\
        0 & I + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda AA^\top
    \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
        I & \gamma_x A^\top \\ 0 & I
    \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
in which the Schur complement $S = I + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda AA^\top$ appears.
Solving \Cref{eq:linear-system-step} therefore amounts to the positive definite system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psd-system}
    \left(I + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda A A^\top\right)\nu = \lambda-\gamma_\lambda (b - Ax)
\end{equation}
for $\nu$, followed by $\xi = x - \gamma_xA^\top\nu$.
The eigenvalues of $S$ are $1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_i(A)^2$ for $i = 1, \ldots, r$, and $1$ with multiplicity $m - r$.
If $A$ has full row rank, the condition number of $S$ is
\begin{equation*}
    \kappa(S) = \frac{1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\max}(A)^2}{1 + \gamma_x\gamma_\lambda\sigma_{\min}(A)^2},
\end{equation*}
which increases from $1$ toward $\kappa(A)^2$ as the product $\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda$ grows.
This is the trade-off behind the step-size sweeps of \Cref{sec:numerical-results}: a larger product $\gamma_x\gamma_\lambda$ often reduces the number of outer iterations while making each inner solve harder.

When \Cref{eq:psd-system} is solved iteratively and $\xi$ is then set to $x - \gamma_x A^\top\nu$, the first block of the residual \Cref{eq:residual-definition} vanishes identically, and the second block is minus the residual of \Cref{eq:psd-system}.
Since $M$ is block diagonal, the relative-error test then involves only the Schur-complement residual $r_S = \lambda - \gamma_\lambda(b - Ax) - S\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psd-residual-in-M-norm}
    \|\varepsilon\|_M^2 = \gamma_\lambda^{-1}\|r_S\|^2,
\end{equation}
which is what the inner solvers of \Cref{sec:numerical-results} monitor after every iteration.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We proposed and analyzed a relaxed Douglas--Rachford method for linearly constrained convex optimization in which the linear system is solved inexactly up to a relative-error test.
We showed that the squared fixed-point residual is summable, so that its running minimum decays as $o(1/k)$, and that the method converges linearly when $f$ is smooth and strongly convex.
We then bounded the inner work: the expected number of inner iterations at each outer iteration is independent of the iteration count, and the total inner work over $n$ outer iterations is a constant times $n$ plus a logarithm of the progress made, with all constants explicit in the tolerance, the relaxation, the contraction factor of the inner solver, and the spectrum of the linear system.
The experiments confirm the three points the analysis makes: on the quadratic families the outer iteration is insensitive to how, and how accurately, the linear system is solved; the inner work per outer iteration settles to a constant that depends on the solver, the tolerance, and the conditioning of the Schur complement but not on the iteration count; and the shared step size trades outer iterations against inner work, with the total inner work minimized at an interior value.

Several directions remain.
The per-iteration bound of \Cref{rem:per-iteration} depends on the ratio of consecutive exact step norms, which we have not bounded from below; the parameters $\gamma_x$, $\gamma_\lambda$, $\sigma$, and $\theta$ were chosen by sweeps or by a simple adaptive rule, and a principled selection is open.
It is also open whether a more flexible inner solver, or an amortization strategy that checks the relative-error test only every few inner iterations, can reduce the time spent in the inner solves.
Finally, the observation that the inexactness barely affects the outer iteration on quadratic programs, while it does on linear programs, deserves an explanation.
